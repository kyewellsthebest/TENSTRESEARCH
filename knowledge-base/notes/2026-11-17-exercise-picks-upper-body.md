# Exercise picks, upper body — named exercises in rank order for every muscle

Run date: 2026-11-17 · Status: researched (needs audit) · Studies file:
`2026-11-17-exercise-picks-upper-body-studies.csv` (48 new rows). Studies already in `knowledge-base/STUDIES.csv` are
cited by their existing keys and are not repeated in the new file.

This note follows on from `2026-11-16-exercise-selection-upper-body.md`. That note asked "does exercise choice matter?".
This one answers "which exact exercises, in what order, set up how?". Every muscle below ends in a ranked list of
named exercises.

## How to read the grades

- **A — won or tied a head-to-head training study.** People trained for weeks, two exercises were compared, and
  muscle size was measured. I say what it beat or tied, and by how much.
- **B — shown to grow the muscle, or backed by a tested principle in this muscle.** A training study measured growth
  of this muscle from this exercise, but there was no head-to-head win. Or the exercise applies something that has
  trial support in that same muscle.
- **C — mechanism or EMG only.** Anatomy, line of pull, or electrodes measuring muscle activity in one session.
  **No growth data.** An EMG difference is not proof of more growth.
- **D — popular but shown inferior, or redundant.**

Rank follows the grade first, then the size of the effect, then practical things: how heavy you can load it, how
stable it is, joint comfort and time. Where two exercises cannot be separated I write **"no data separates these"**.

**Three warnings before you read the tables.**

1. Grade A does not mean "proven best". It means "this exercise has been in a real growth trial and did not lose".
   Most of those trials used beginners for 8–12 weeks.
2. For lats, mid-back, traps, rear delts and forearms there is **still no head-to-head growth study**. Those lists are
   mostly grade C. The order inside grade C is my judgment from EMG and practicality.
3. EMG reads lower when a muscle is stretched, whatever the effort. So EMG is biased against stretched-position
   exercises (overhead extensions, incline curls, flyes). I do not use EMG to rank those against other exercises.

Access is marked FULL (full text read), ABSTRACT or SECONDARY. Unless I say otherwise, every study below was read as
an abstract.

## 1. Chest — upper (clavicular) pec

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Low-incline press, bench at about 30–45°** — barbell, Smith machine or dumbbells; bar or hands come down to the upper chest | The only upper-pec exercise with a head-to-head win. The upper-pec site grew 0.62 cm more than with flat bench (95% CI 0.23 to 1.0). Beginners, once a week. EMG agrees: upper-pec activity peaks at 30–45° | A | Chaves2020 (ABSTRACT); RodriguezRidao2020, Trebs2010, Coratella2020b (EMG, ABSTRACT) |
| 2 | **Pec deck (machine fly)** — seat set so the hands travel at mid-chest height, let the arms go back to a full stretch | Upper head +17–18% in 12 weeks in beginners. No comparison exercise | B | Pinto2025 (ABSTRACT) |
| 3 | **Flat barbell bench press** | Grows the whole pec on MRI, but the upper site grew less than with incline | B | Lanza2024, Chaves2020 (ABSTRACT) |
| 4 | **Low-to-high cable crossover** — pulleys at the bottom, hands finish together at chin height | Line of pull runs along the upper-pec fibres. That is all the support it has. No EMG or growth study found | C | none found |
| 5 | **Incline dumbbell fly, bench at about 30°** | Same reasoning as rank 4, with a deep stretch. No study found | C | none found |

**Also fine**

- Incline machine press or incline Smith press. Barbell and machine pressing grew the pecs equally in trained men
  (HernandezBelmonte2023), and an incline machine press was part of the programme in Wolf2025. Nobody measured the upper
  pec in either.
- Flat press + incline press in the same week. Fine, as long as the incline gets real sets. In Chaves2020 the
  "both" group split the same number of sets between the two and grew the upper site 0.50 cm less than incline alone.

**Skip or demote**

- **Incline steeper than about 45°.** EMG shows the front delt taking over and pec activity falling
  (RodriguezRidao2020). Grade C evidence.
- **Seated machine chest press as your upper-pec exercise.** Lowest upper-pec EMG of four presses in 10 bodybuilders
  (Coratella2020b). Grade C evidence. It is fine for the mid/lower pec.
- **Decline press for the upper pec.** Upper-pec EMG was lower than in the incline press (Coratella2020b).

**Evidence notes**

- Chaves2020: 47 untrained men, 8 weeks, one session a week, flat vs incline vs both, sets equated. Only 1 of 3 pec
  sites differed. **The incline angle is not in the abstract**, and the full text was unreachable again today (Europe
  PMC HTTP 500). The 30–45° advice comes from EMG, not from this trial.
- EMG: upper-pec activity was highest at 30° (RodriguezRidao2020, n = 30), at 44° (Trebs2010, n = 15) and at 45° vs
  flat, decline and machine (Coratella2020b, n = 10 bodybuilders, d = 2.78–7.80).
- Pinto2025: 15 untrained men, pec deck only, 12 weeks. Clavicular head +17% (1 set) and +18% (3 sets).
- Nobody has tested incline vs flat in trained lifters, dumbbell vs barbell incline, or any cable or fly movement for
  the upper pec.

## 2. Chest — mid and lower (sternal) pec

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Flat barbell bench press, medium grip** | Tied the machine version for pec growth in trained men (difference ≤2.0%, P ≥ 0.208) and the Smith version in students. Bench-only training grew the pec on MRI. Heaviest loading | A | HernandezBelmonte2023, MarcosFrutos2025, Lanza2024 (ABSTRACT) |
| 1= | **Machine chest press** or **Smith-machine flat bench** | Tied the barbell in the same two trials. No data separates these from rank 1; pick by shoulder comfort | A | HernandezBelmonte2023, MarcosFrutos2025 |
| 3 | **Pec deck (machine fly)**, full stretch | Sternal head +21% in 12 weeks in beginners. No comparison exercise | B | Pinto2025 |
| 4 | **Flat dumbbell bench press** | Never tested for growth. Pec EMG equal to barbell and Smith in one study and higher than barbell in another. Loads are about 17% lighter and the triceps do less | C | Saeterbakken2011, Farias2017, Welsch2005 |
| 5 | **Weighted dip on parallel bars**, slight forward lean | No growth data for the chest. The only study I found describes joint angles and triceps EMG | C | McKenzie2022 |
| 6 | **High-to-low cable crossover** or **flat dumbbell fly** | No growth data. One EMG study found 8–81% lower pec, delt and triceps activity in the fly than the bench at a 6RM load; another found equal pec amplitude but less time under activation | C | Solstad2020, Welsch2005 |

**Also fine**

- **Loaded push-up.** Tied the bench press for pec growth at a matched light load (Kikuchi2017, n = 18, 40% 1RM), and
  a band-loaded 6RM push-up matched the bench for strength in trained students (Calatayud2015). Grade A as a tie, but
  at 87 kg and intermediate–advanced it is hard to load heavily enough. Use it as a finisher or when travelling.
- **Decline bench, about −15°.** Sternal-pec EMG equal to flat, with less front-delt activity (Coratella2020b). No
  growth data. It adds nothing a flat press does not already do.

**Skip or demote**

- **Decline press as a special "lower-chest builder".** No growth study, and EMG shows no advantage over flat. D
  (redundant).
- **Dumbbell or barbell pullover for chest.** EMG shows the pecs working (Marchetti2011, Muyor2022), but only with
  light loads, and there is no growth data.
- **Loaded stretching between sets.** In 26 trained men, adding a 30-second loaded pec stretch in every rest period
  changed nothing (pec thickness +2.7 vs +3.0 mm; Wadhi2022). D (redundant).
- **Changing grip width to "hit the pecs differently".** Pec EMG was the same at narrow, medium and wide grips
  (Saeterbakken2021).

**Evidence notes**

- HernandezBelmonte2023: 38 trained men, 8 weeks, barbell vs machine versions of bench, squat, prone row and shoulder
  press. Pec cross-sectional area grew in both; difference ≤2.0%.
- MarcosFrutos2025: 37 students, 8 weeks, free-weight vs Smith bench. Pec area improved "comparably"; no numbers in
  the abstract.
- Lanza2024: bench press only, 13 trainees vs 11 controls, 10 weeks, MRI. Pecs grew more than every other muscle
  measured.
- Pinto2025: sternocostal head +21% with either 1 or 3 sets of pec deck.
- Dumbbell press EMG: equal pec activity to barbell and Smith, 17% lighter load, less triceps (Saeterbakken2011,
  n = 12). More total reps (31.2 vs 27.8) and more pec EMG than barbell (Farias2017, n = 19).
- Stretch as a pec stimulus is mixed. Fifteen minutes a day of static pec stretching grew the pec as much as lifting
  3 days a week in 81 active adults (Wohlann2024). The 30-second inter-set version did nothing in trained men
  (Wadhi2022). Neither tells you a fly beats a press.
- Bench vs fly, cable crossover, dips or dumbbell press has still never been tested for growth.

## 3. Lats

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lat pulldown to the front** — overhand or neutral grip, hands about shoulder-width to 1.5× shoulder-width, bar to the upper chest | A programme built on pulldowns and seated rows grew lat muscle fibres in 18 beginners in 8 weeks. Front beats behind-the-neck for lat EMG while pulling. Grip width makes no difference to lat EMG | B | Paoli2016 (FULL, part read); Padovan2024, Andersen2014, Buonsenso2025 (EMG) |
| 2 | **Seated cable row, narrow neutral handle, elbows tight to the ribs** | The other half of the Paoli2016 programme. A narrow grip gave more lat EMG than a wide grip (ES 1.08, n = 14) | B | Paoli2016; Padovan2025b (EMG, ABSTRACT) |
| 3 | **Pull-up (overhand) or chin-up (underhand)**, add weight once you pass about 10 reps | Lat EMG is very high and the same across grips. No lat difference from the pulldown was reported. **No data separates this from rank 1**; it sits third only because nobody has measured lat growth from it | C | Youdas2010, Dickie2017, Doma2013, DiFonza2026 (FULL) |
| 4 | **Straight-arm cable pulldown** — bar or rope, arms nearly straight, pull from eye level to the thighs | Lat EMG higher than in the barbell pullover at a light load (n = 20). Trains the lats without the biceps limiting you | C | Muyor2022 (ABSTRACT) |
| 5 | **Single-arm cable pulldown** or **one-arm dumbbell row** | Both were in the programme of Wolf2025 (trained lifters), but the lats were not measured. No lat data | C | Wolf2025 (exercise list read in full text) |

**Also fine**

- **Inverted row** (body under a bar or Smith bar). Highest lat EMG of three rows with the lowest spine load, but in 7
  men (Fenwick2009).
- **Machine pulldown or plate-loaded high row.** No study. Same joint action as rank 1.
- **Underhand vs overhand pulldown.** One study found more lat EMG overhand (Lusk2010, n = 12). A larger one found no
  difference across seven variants (Buonsenso2025, n = 40). Use whichever your elbows like.

**Skip or demote**

- **Dumbbell or barbell pullover as a lat exercise.** EMG shows the pecs doing more than the lats (Marchetti2011,
  n = 8; Muyor2022, n = 20). C evidence, but it all points one way. No study on machine or cable pullovers was found.
- **Behind-the-neck pulldown.** Less lat EMG in the pulling phase than the front version (Padovan2024) and an awkward
  shoulder position. No benefit shown.
- **Very wide grip.** No extra lat EMG and a lower 6RM (77.3 vs about 80 kg; Andersen2014).

**Evidence notes**

- **There is still no trial comparing two back exercises for lat size.** I searched again today (PubMed, Europe PMC,
  SportRxiv through Crossref) and found none. DiFonza2026 (review of 23 lat EMG studies, FULL) says the same.
- Paoli2016 (FULL, methods and results paragraphs): 18 novice men, 8 weeks. Exercises: bench press, lat pulldown,
  seated row, shoulder press, hammer curl, lying external rotation. Lat single-fibre area rose (p < 0.05) and
  pulldown 1RM rose 17%. It was a protein study, so there is no exercise comparison, and pulldown and row cannot be
  separated. See the audit flag on what its MRI measured.
- Pull-up EMG: lats 117–130% of the reference contraction in pull-up, chin-up and rotating-handle versions (Youdas2010,
  n = 25). Values over 100% mean the reference contraction was not truly maximal. Similar activity across four grips
  (Dickie2017, n = 19).
- Seated-row grip: narrow favours the lats, wide favours the traps (Padovan2025b). DiFonza2026 warns this is specific
  to the seated row. In the bent-over row a wider grip gave more lat EMG (one small study, SECONDARY via DiFonza2026).

## 4. Upper and mid back — mid traps, lower traps, rhomboids

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seated cable row, wide overhand grip, elbows out, bar to the lower chest** | Mid-trap/rhomboid EMG was higher in the seated row than in pulldowns. A wide grip gave more upper, mid and lower trap EMG than a narrow grip (ES 0.71–2.79) | C | Lehman2004, Padovan2025b |
| 2 | **Chest-supported row, full range** — prone barbell row on a bench, T-bar with chest pad, or machine | Trunk is supported, so the back muscles, not the lower back, set the limit. Cutting the range to the top half lowered peak mid-trap EMG (d = 0.42–0.54) | C | Fischer2025 |
| 3 | **Pull-up, overhand grip** | Mid-trap EMG higher than with a neutral grip (60 vs 37% of maximum). More lower-trap EMG than the chin-up | C | Dickie2017, Youdas2010 |
| 4 | **Barbell bent-over row** | "Large, symmetrical" back muscle activity, but the highest spine load of three rows (n = 7) | C | Fenwick2009 |
| 5 | **Prone Y-raise** (face down on an incline bench, arms raised overhead in a Y) and **reverse fly** | Highest lower-trap and mid-trap EMG among 10 rehab-style exercises (n = 30). Light loads only | C | Ekstrom2003 |

No data separates ranks 1–4. Pick the row you can load hard without your lower back giving out first.

**Also fine**

- **Inverted row.** Highest upper-back EMG of three rows with the lowest spine load (Fenwick2009, n = 7).
- **One-arm dumbbell row, T-bar row without chest support, machine row.** No EMG or growth data separates them.
- **Face pull.** No EMG or growth study found. It is a wide, elbows-out row with a rope, so it probably behaves like
  rank 1 with a lighter load. That is a guess from mechanics.

**Skip or demote**

- **Counting pulldowns as mid-back work.** Mid-trap/rhomboid EMG was lower than in the seated row (Lehman2004).
- **Fussing over "squeezing the shoulder blades".** Deliberate retraction did not raise mid-trap/rhomboid EMG
  (Lehman2004). Fixed vs free shoulder blades gave similar overall activity (Padovan2025a, n = 14).
- **Top-half-only rows.** Lower peak mid-trap EMG than full range (Fischer2025).

**Evidence notes**

- No growth study exists for the mid or lower traps or the rhomboids. Everything here is EMG from 7–30 people.
- Lehman2004 measured 10-second isometric holds, not moving reps. Its sample size (12) comes from DiFonza2026.
- Padovan2025b: 14 trained men, 8RM, high-density EMG. Wide grip: upper trap ES 1.35 (pulling) and 2.79 (lowering),
  mid trap 1.24 and 1.44, lower trap 0.90 and 0.71.

## 5. Upper traps

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dumbbell shrug**, heavy, arms at the sides, one arm at a time or both | Highest upper-trap EMG of 10 exercises (n = 30), and 102% of maximum in a second study. Easy to load | C | Ekstrom2003, Andersen2008 |
| 2 | **Shrug with the arms about 30° out to the sides** — wide-grip barbell, trap bar, or cables set wide | More upper-trap and lower-trap EMG than the arms-at-sides shrug (n = 37). Rehab loads | C | Pizzari2014 |
| 3 | **Lateral raise** (already in your side-delt work) | Upper-trap EMG 97% of maximum with 3–10 kg, close to the shrug's 102% with 20–30 kg | C | Andersen2008 |
| 4 | **Wide-grip upright row** | Upper-trap EMG 85% of maximum; a wider grip raised trap activity | C | Andersen2008, McAllister2013 |

**Also fine**

- Barbell shrug, machine shrug, trap-bar shrug. No study separates any shrug from any other.

**Skip or demote**

- **Narrow-grip upright row.** Less delt and trap EMG than a wide grip (McAllister2013).
- **Extra shrug volume on top of a full programme, if time is short.** Lateral raises and wide rows already load the
  upper traps hard on EMG (Andersen2008, Padovan2025b). Whether shrugs add growth on top is untested.

**Evidence notes**

- No study has measured upper-trap growth from shrugs, or from any named exercise.
- Strength training does grow the upper trap: fibre area rose 36% in 9 women after 10 weeks (Kadi2000). The abstract
  does not name the exercises, so no exercise gets the credit.
- Andersen2008 tested 12 women with chronic neck pain. Treat the percentages as rough.

## 6. Shoulders — front delt

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Flat or low-incline bench press** (already in your chest work) | Bench-only training grew the front delt on MRI | B | Lanza2024 |
| 2 | **Overhead press** — seated dumbbell or standing/seated barbell, bar in front of the head | Highest front-delt EMG of the presses: 33.3% vs 21.4% for the bench. No growth data. No data separates this from rank 1 | C | Campos2020, Padovan2026 |
| 3 | **Machine shoulder press** | Same movement. Lower overall muscle activity than the barbell in 8 bodybuilders. No growth data | C | Coratella2022 |
| 4 | **Front raise** (dumbbell, plate or cable) | Works the front delt and pec on EMG, but pressing already grows the front delt. No growth data | D (redundant) | Coratella2020, Lanza2024 |

**Also fine**

- Incline press at 45–60°. Front-delt EMG rises with every step of incline (RodriguezRidao2020, Trebs2010).

**Skip or demote**

- **Front raises**, unless you do no pressing at all.
- **Decline press** if front delts are a priority: lowest front-delt EMG of four presses (Coratella2020b).

**Evidence notes**

- Lanza2024: front-delt cross-sectional area grew more than in controls after 10 weeks of bench pressing only.
- Front vs behind-the-neck press: two studies from the same lab disagree on details (Coratella2022, n = 8;
  Padovan2026, n = 14). The larger one found more front-delt EMG with the bar in front (ES 1.0).
- HernandezBelmonte2023 trained the shoulder press (barbell vs machine) but did not measure the delts.

## 7. Shoulders — side delt

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dumbbell lateral raise** — arm from the side up to shoulder height, elbow almost straight, upper arm not twisted (thumb level, not up) | Tied the cable version in trained lifters: +3.3–4.6% thickness in 8 weeks, differences −0.25 mm and +0.20 mm, Bayes factors 0.27 and 0.22 (support for "equal") | A | Larsen2025 (FULL) |
| 1= | **Cable lateral raise, pulley at hand height**, cable crossing in front of the body, same range | The other arm of the same trial. No data separates these | A | Larsen2025 (FULL) |
| 3 | **Overhead press** (barbell or dumbbell) | Side-delt EMG 27.9% of maximum vs 30.3% in the lateral raise. No growth data | C | Campos2020 |
| 4 | **Wide-grip upright row** — hands about twice shoulder-width, bar to the lower chest | A wider grip raised side-delt EMG (n = 16). No growth data | C | McAllister2013 |
| 5 | **Machine lateral raise** | No study found. Same joint action as rank 1 | C | none found |

**Also fine**

- **Lean-away cable raise** and **behind-the-body cable raise.** No study found on either. Both shift the hardest
  point toward the bottom of the rep. Larsen2025 tested exactly that idea (cable hardest at the bottom vs dumbbell
  hardest at the top) and found no difference. So they are fine, but there is no reason to expect more.
- **Wide-grip seated row.** Raised side-delt EMG compared with a narrow grip (ES 1.03; Padovan2025b). A small bonus,
  not a replacement.

**Skip or demote**

- **Bench press as side-delt work.** D. The side delt grew least of every muscle measured after bench-only training
  (Lanza2024), and its EMG in the bench was 5% of maximum (Campos2020).
- **Thumbs-up (arm turned outward) or bent-elbow lateral raises.** Lower side-delt EMG than the neutral version (ES
  1.47 and 6.41; Coratella2020, n = 10 bodybuilders). C evidence.
- **Thumbs-down "pouring" raises.** Raised rear-delt and upper-trap EMG, not shown to raise side-delt EMG
  (Coratella2020). One review ranks this version top for the side delt (Rabello2024). The two disagree. No growth data.
- **Front raise for side delts.** Far lower side-delt EMG (ES 10.28; Coratella2020).
- **Machine shoulder press for side delts.** Lower side-delt EMG than barbell pressing (Coratella2020b for chest
  press machine vs bench; Coratella2022 for shoulder press). C evidence.

**Evidence notes**

- Larsen2025 (FULL): 24 trained lifters (about 7 years), one arm per exercise, 5 sets twice a week to failure. The
  cable was set level with the hand so it pulled hardest with the arm at the side. Funded by an equipment company
  (Jaquish Biomedical).
- Overhead press vs lateral raise has never been tested for growth.
- Behind-the-neck press: more side-delt EMG than the front press in 8 bodybuilders (ES 3.68; Coratella2022), but no
  amplitude difference in 14 trained men (Padovan2026). Not a pick.

## 8. Shoulders — rear delt

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reverse pec deck, neutral grip** (palms facing each other, vertical handles) | More rear-delt EMG than the seated row or pulldown (n = 12). Neutral grip beat palms-down by 0.1–7.4% of maximum (n = 19) | C | Franke2015, Schoenfeld2013 |
| 2 | **Reverse cable fly** or **bent-over dumbbell reverse fly** | Same joint action as rank 1. No study separates them | C | none found for a direct comparison |
| 3 | **Pull-ups** and **inverted rows** | Ranked highest for the rear delt in a review of 33 EMG studies. This disagrees with Franke2015. No data settles it | C | Rabello2024 |
| 4 | **Wide-grip row to the chest**, or **wide overhand pulldown leaning back about 30°** | The lean-back pulldown gave more rear-delt EMG than the upright one (p = 0.011, n = 40). Rows with fixed shoulder blades gave more rear-delt EMG (ES 0.66) | C | Buonsenso2025, Padovan2025a |
| 5 | **Face pull** | No EMG or growth study found. Mechanics only | C | none found |

**Also fine**

- **Lateral raise.** Rear-delt EMG was 24% of maximum in the lateral raise against 11.4% in the shoulder press
  (Campos2020). Your side-delt work helps a little.

**Skip or demote**

- **Relying on the bench or shoulder press.** Rear-delt EMG 3.5% and 11.4% of maximum (Campos2020).
- **Palms-down grip on the reverse pec deck.** Slightly lower rear-delt EMG than neutral (Schoenfeld2013).

**Evidence notes**

- **No growth study exists for the rear delt.** All of this is EMG from 12–40 people, and the two best sources
  disagree on whether rows and pull-ups are enough (Franke2015 vs Rabello2024).
- Given that, the sensible move is one direct rear-delt exercise (rank 1 or 2) plus your normal rows.

## 9. Biceps brachii (long and short head)

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Incline dumbbell curl** — lie back on an incline bench, upper arms hanging straight down behind the body, palms up, arm fully straight at the bottom | Beat the preacher curl for the biceps itself: +22.1% vs +18.9% muscle volume (g = 0.33; MRI, 12 weeks, 21 beginners). In a second trial the site nearest the shoulder grew 0.08 cm more (95% CI 0.02 to 0.13) | A | Kobayashi2026, Kassiano2025 (ABSTRACT) |
| 2 | **Preacher curl** — dumbbell, EZ-bar or barbell; let the arm go fully straight at the bottom of every rep | Lost to the incline curl by about 3 points for the biceps, but grew the lower biceps 0.10 cm more (0.04 to 0.15). Bottom-half reps grew 8.9% vs 3.4% for top-half reps. Barbell and cable versions tied (+8% vs +7%) | A | Kobayashi2026, Kassiano2025, Sato2021, Nunes2020a (FULL) |
| 3 | **Standing dumbbell curl, palms up** — alternating or both arms together | Beat the dumbbell row two to one (+11.06% vs +5.16%, p = 0.009). One arm at a time and both together tied (difference 0.02 cm, −0.10 to 0.15; n = 54). Strict and "cheat" reps tied. Part of the programme that grew trained lifters' arms | A | Mannarino2021, Kassiano2026b, Augustin2025, Wolf2025 |
| 4 | **Barbell curl** or **EZ-bar curl**, standing | Tied the wide-grip pulldown for biceps growth in beginners (+5.83% vs +6.10%). Straight bar vs EZ bar: 1.8% EMG difference, which is nothing | A (tie) | Gentil2015 (FULL); Coratella2023b, Marcolin2018 (EMG) |
| 5 | **Cable curl** — arm at the side, or arm behind the body ("Bayesian" curl, facing away from a low pulley) | The two tied (−0.40 mm, 95% CrI −1.06 to 0.26, and +0.21 mm, −0.25 to 0.65). Bayesian vs preacher-style cable curl: +9% vs +7% (p = 0.314). The extra stretch added nothing measurable | A (tie) | Larsen2026 (FULL), Attarieh2025 (FULL) |
| 6 | **Chin-up (underhand)** or **lat pulldown** | A wide-grip pulldown tied the barbell curl in beginners (Gentil2015). Chin-ups give more biceps EMG than pull-ups or pulldowns. Counts toward biceps; untested as a replacement for curls in trained lifters | A (pulldown, tie); C (chin-up) | Gentil2015; Youdas2010, Doma2013 (EMG) |

**No data separates ranks 3, 4 and 5 from each other, or from ranks 1–2.** The incline and preacher curls lead
because they have the best measurements (MRI) and each has a small proven edge in one region. Total elbow-flexor
growth was the same for the two (+17.6% vs +19.2%, P = 0.134).

**Long head vs short head.** No training study has measured the two heads separately. Kobayashi2026 measured the
biceps as one muscle. I found nothing on wide vs narrow grip for either head. Any claim that an exercise "builds the
peak" or "hits the short head" is untested.

**Also fine**

- **Cable preacher curl, machine preacher curl.** The cable version tied the barbell version (Nunes2020a). No study
  on the machine; assume the same.
- **Spider curl** (chest on an incline bench, arms hanging in front). No study found. It puts the arm in front of the
  body like a preacher curl, and arm position by itself did not change growth (Attarieh2025, Larsen2026).
- **Concentration curl.** Highest biceps EMG of four curls at a light load (Croskery2026, n = 15). EMG only.
- **Bottom-half ("lengthened partial") preacher curls.** Tied full-range reps in 13 trained lifters (SMD 0.10 at the
  lower site; Havers2025). No better, no worse.

**Skip or demote**

- **Top-half-only curls** (stopping well short of straight). D. Grew 3.4% against 8.9% for bottom-half reps
  (Sato2021, n = 24), and less at the lower biceps (Pedrosa2023, n = 19).
- **Rows as your biceps work.** D. Half the growth of curls (Mannarino2021), and less biceps swelling straight after
  training (Leitao2024, acute only).
- **Hammer curls and reverse curls as biceps exercises.** Biceps EMG was 12% and 19% lower than with palms up
  (Coratella2023a), and the hammer curl ranked last of four (Croskery2026). C evidence. No growth data either way.
- **The Bayesian curl as an upgrade.** It tied, twice. It also showed lower biceps EMG than a dumbbell curl (Parpa2025,
  n = 11), though EMG is biased against stretched positions. Use it if you like it.
- **Swapping bars to chase EMG.** Straight vs EZ vs dumbbell differences are small and inconsistent (Marcolin2018,
  Coratella2023b). Choose by wrist comfort.

**Evidence notes**

- Kobayashi2026: 21 untrained adults, one arm per curl, 5 × 10 at 70% 1RM twice a week, 12 weeks, MRI. The abstract
  does not give the bench angle.
- Kassiano2025: 63 young women, 8 weeks. Differences of about one millimetre.
- ZabaletaKorta2023: 38 recreationally trained women, 9 weeks. Only the preacher group's lowest site grew, but the
  between-group test was not significant (p = 0.367).
- Sato2021: 32 untrained adults, 5 weeks, seated dumbbell preacher curl, 0–50° vs 80–130° of elbow bend. Short and
  small. Pedrosa2023: 19 untrained women, 8 weeks, same exercise, 0–68° vs 68–135°; only the lower site differed
  (p = 0.001).
- Havers2025: 13 trained lifters (about 6 years), 8 weeks, one arm per condition. Trivial differences.
- Mannarino2021: 10 untrained men, one arm rows, one arm curls, 8 weeks.
- Kassiano2026b: 54 untrained women, 8 weeks, 2 sets three times a week. A correction to this paper exists that I
  could not read.
- Augustin2025: 30 untrained adults, 8 weeks, strict vs body-momentum dumbbell curls and cable pushdowns. Bayes
  factors 0.06–0.61 for "no difference".
- Shoulder position, trained lifters: 21 trained men, 3 weeks of light blood-flow-restricted curls, arm behind vs in
  front: no difference at any of three MRI sites (Vendruscolo2025). Short and unusual, but it agrees with the rest.
- Lengthened partials across the whole body: 297 people at 15 gyms, 12 weeks, arm muscle area estimate: no meaningful
  difference from full range (Gschneidner2025).

## 10. Brachialis (under the biceps; adds arm width)

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Preacher curl** — dumbbell or EZ-bar, palms up, full straighten at the bottom | Beat the incline curl for the brachialis: +21.1% vs +17.1% volume (g = 0.50) | A | Kobayashi2026 |
| 2 | **Incline dumbbell curl** | Lost by 4 points but still grew the brachialis 17.1% | B | Kobayashi2026 |
| 3 | **Cable curl**, any arm position | Brachialis thickness +10% (preacher-style) and +8% (Bayesian), no difference | B | Attarieh2025 (FULL) |
| 4 | **Hammer curl** (palms facing each other) | Mechanics only: turning the hand takes work off the biceps, so the brachialis should do more. Surface EMG cannot measure the brachialis, and no growth study exists | C | Coratella2023a (biceps EMG only) |
| 5 | **Reverse curl** (palms down, EZ-bar) | Same reasoning; biceps EMG fell 19% | C | Coratella2023a |

**Also fine**

- **One-arm dumbbell row.** Brachialis swelling straight after training was the same after rows and curls (Leitao2024,
  16 trained men). Acute only. Rows probably do more for the brachialis than for the biceps.

**Skip or demote**

- **Adding hammer curls "for the brachialis" on top of preacher curls** when time is short. The preacher curl is the
  only exercise with proof of brachialis growth. Hammer curls may well work; nobody has measured it.

**Evidence notes**

- Only Kobayashi2026 (MRI), Attarieh2025 and Sato2021 (ultrasound, biceps + brachialis together) have measured the
  brachialis after training. All used beginners.
- Paoli2016's programme used hammer curls as its only curl and arm muscle area rose, but pulldowns and rows were in
  the same programme, so it proves nothing about hammer curls.

## 11. Brachioradialis (top of the forearm, thumb side)

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Preacher curl** — dumbbell or EZ-bar, palms up | Beat the incline curl: +17.6% vs +13.5% volume (g = 0.47) | A | Kobayashi2026 |
| 2 | **Incline dumbbell curl** | Lost by 4 points but grew it 13.5% | B | Kobayashi2026 |
| 3 | **EZ-bar or barbell curl, palms up** | Brachioradialis EMG higher than with dumbbells, and higher palms-up than with a hammer or reverse grip (+6% and +5%) | C | Marcolin2018, Coratella2023a |
| 4 | **Hammer curl** and **reverse curl** | The usual gym picks for this muscle. No growth data, and the one EMG study in bodybuilders went the other way | C | Coratella2023a |

**Also fine**

- **Pull-ups and chin-ups.** The brachioradialis is active in all grips (Dickie2017). No numbers in the abstract.

**Skip or demote**

- **Buying into "reverse curls build the forearm" as a fact.** It is untested. Ten bodybuilders showed slightly
  *lower* brachioradialis EMG with a reverse grip (Coratella2023a). That is one small EMG study, so the honest answer
  is "unknown", not "useless".

**Evidence notes**

- Kobayashi2026 is the only training study that measured the brachioradialis.
- Coratella2023a: 10 competitive bodybuilders, 8RM load, non-fatiguing sets.

## 12. Triceps — long head (the big inner part; most of the triceps' size)

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Overhead cable extension** — rope or bar, facing away from the pulley, upper arms up beside the head, elbows bent fully at the bottom | Beat the arm-at-side cable extension: long head +28.5% vs +19.6% (d = 0.61; MRI, 12 weeks, n = 21), while using 34–39% lighter loads. A 9-person pilot leaned the same way (+13.7% vs +10.7%, not significant) | A | Maeo2023, Stasinaki2018 (ABSTRACT) |
| 2 | **Lying EZ-bar or barbell triceps extension (skull-crusher)** — lower the bar behind the top of the head, not to the forehead | Lying extensions grew the long head more than the bench press alone (MRI, 43 men). No numbers in the abstract. Lowering behind the head stretches the long head more; that part is mechanics only | A (vs bench press); C for the behind-the-head detail | Brandao2020 (ABSTRACT) |
| 3 | **Overhead dumbbell extension** — one dumbbell in both hands, or one arm at a time, seated | Same arm position as rank 1, and part of the programme that grew trained lifters' triceps. Not tested against the cable version | B | Wolf2025, Maeo2023 |
| 4 | **Cable pushdown** — rope or bar, elbows at the sides | Grew the long head 19.6%, but lost to the overhead version by 9 points | B (lost head-to-head) | Maeo2023, Augustin2025, Wolf2025 |
| 5 | **Bench press, close-grip bench press, dips** | The bench grew the long head less than extensions did. No growth data for close-grip bench or dips | C | Brandao2020, McKenzie2022 |

**No study has compared rank 1 with rank 2.** Rank 1 leads because its win comes with numbers.

**Also fine**

- **Machine overhead extension, seated EZ-bar overhead extension ("French press").** No study; same position as rank 1.
- **Lying dumbbell extension.** Same EMG pattern as the overhead dumbbell extension in 21 trained men (Alves2018), so
  doing both in one session is redundant.

**Skip or demote**

- **Pushdowns as your only triceps exercise.** They work, but they lost on every head (Maeo2023).
- **Kickbacks.** No growth study found. The arm is behind the body, which shortens the long head. That is the opposite
  of the position that won in Maeo2023. Mechanics only, but it points the wrong way, and the load is tiny.
- **Relying on pressing for the long head.** D for this purpose (Brandao2020).
- **JM press.** No study of any kind found.

**Evidence notes**

- Maeo2023: 21 adults, one arm per position, cable, 5 × 10 at 70% 1RM twice a week, 12 weeks, MRI. Whole triceps
  +19.9% vs +13.9% (d = 0.54). One study, cable only, and the abstract does not state training status
  (`top5/F3-findings.md` records them as untrained from a secondary source).
- Stasinaki2018: 9 novice women, 6 weeks, cable pushdown with one arm and overhead cable extension with the other,
  6 × 6RM. Thickness +10.7% vs +13.7% at one site and +15.5% vs +19.4% at the other. Too small to separate them.
- Brandao2020: bench only vs lying barbell triceps extension only vs both in either order. The abstract gives no
  magnitudes, trial length or training status, and does not say where the bar was lowered to.
- Wolf2025 (trained lifters, 8 weeks): the programme used dumbbell overhead extensions and cable pushdowns, and
  elbow-extensor thickness grew. It cannot say which exercise did what.
- Goto2019 is often quoted for mid-range partial triceps reps in trained men (+48.7% vs +28.2% in 8 weeks). Growth
  that large in trained men is not believable next to every other study. I give it no weight.

## 13. Triceps — lateral and medial heads (the outer "horseshoe")

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Overhead cable extension** | Beat the arm-at-side version here too: +14.6% vs +10.5% (d = 0.39) | A | Maeo2023 |
| 2 | **Flat barbell bench press** (medium grip; Smith machine also fine) | The lateral head grew more in every bench-press group than with lying extensions only. Barbell and Smith give more triceps EMG than dumbbells (ES 0.62–0.73) | A | Brandao2020; Saeterbakken2011, Farias2017 (EMG) |
| 3 | **Cable pushdown**, rope or bar | Grew these heads 10.5%; lost to overhead by 4 points | B (lost head-to-head) | Maeo2023 |
| 4 | **Skull-crusher** | The whole triceps grew, but the lateral head grew less than in the bench groups | B | Brandao2020 |
| 5 | **Weighted dip on parallel bars**, torso upright | Higher peak triceps EMG than bench dips. No growth data | C | McKenzie2022 |

No study has compared rank 1 with rank 2.

**Also fine**

- **Close-grip bench press.** No growth data. On EMG a narrow grip matched a medium grip for the triceps and lowered
  the 6RM load; only a *wide* grip reduced triceps activity (Saeterbakken2021). So it has no proven edge over your
  normal bench.
- **Machine dip, machine chest press.** No triceps growth data.

**Skip or demote**

- **Bench dips (hands on a bench behind you).** Lower peak triceps EMG than bar dips (0.83 vs 1.04 mV) and more
  shoulder extension (McKenzie2022).
- **Dumbbell bench as triceps work.** Lower triceps EMG than barbell or Smith (Saeterbakken2011, Farias2017).
- **Wide-grip bench as triceps work.** Lower triceps EMG (Saeterbakken2021).

**Evidence notes**

- Shoulder position should not change the length of these two heads, yet they still grew more overhead (Maeo2023).
  So "stretch" is not the whole explanation for that study.
- Lanza2024 and Ogasawara2012 agree that bench pressing alone grows the triceps, but less than it grows the pecs.

## 14. Forearms — wrist flexors and extensors

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Heavy finger-flexor (grip) training** — the study's exercise is not named in its abstract; the closest gym versions are heavy dumbbell or bar holds, dead hangs and thick-handle holds | Forearm circumference +0.6 to +0.8 cm in 5 weeks in 22 climbers. No control group, tape measure only | B (weak) | Andersen2026 |
| 2 | **Wrist curl** — forearm flat on a bench or your thigh, palm up, barbell or dumbbell, let the bar roll toward the fingertips and curl it back | The wrist and finger flexors are most of the forearm's bulk, and this loads them directly. No study found | C | none found |
| 3 | **Reverse wrist curl** — same position, palm down | The only direct loading for the wrist extensors on the top of the forearm. No study found | C | none found |
| 4 | **Preacher or EZ-bar curls** (already in your arm work) | Grow the brachioradialis, the largest muscle on the thumb side (section 11) | A for that muscle only | Kobayashi2026 |

**Also fine**

- Wrist roller, cable wrist curls, behind-the-back barbell wrist curls. No study on any of them.
- Not using straps on some pulling sets. No study; it simply adds grip work.

**Skip or demote**

- **Short maximal squeezes on a gripper.** D for size. Four 5-second maximal squeezes, 18 sessions: grip strength
  +4.8 kg but no change in forearm thickness (Wong2024, 47 people).
- **Long light squeezes (2 minutes at 30%).** No change in strength or size unless blood flow was restricted, and
  then only +0.06 cm (Wong2024).

**Evidence notes**

- **No study compares forearm exercises, and none has measured growth from wrist curls or reverse wrist curls.** I
  searched for both by name and found nothing.
- Andersen2026: pilot, advanced climbers, high-load vs low-load blood-flow-restricted finger-flexor training twice a
  week. Both raised forearm circumference; no group difference.
- Chomiuk2025: 25 recreational athletes, 6 weeks of gym plus climbing, forearm circumference +0.5 to +0.8 cm. No
  control group and no way to separate the two kinds of training.
- So the forearm section is the weakest in this note. Ranks 2 and 3 are there because they are the only exercises
  that load those muscles directly, not because anything proves them.

## Sample week of picks

Two upper-body sessions, each done twice a week (or re-cut as push/pull). It uses 2–3 exercises per muscle across the
week and no redundant ones. For sets, effort and progression see `library/training/top-5-factors.md`; this note only
picks the exercises.

**Upper A**

1. Flat barbell bench press — mid/lower pec, front delt, lateral triceps head.
2. Front lat pulldown, medium overhand grip — lats; also counts toward biceps.
3. Chest-supported row, wide grip, full range — mid traps, rhomboids, rear delt.
4. Dumbbell lateral raise — side delt; also loads the upper traps.
5. Overhead cable rope extension — all three triceps heads, long head most.
6. Incline dumbbell curl — biceps first, brachialis second.
7. Reverse pec deck, neutral grip — rear delt.

**Upper B**

1. Low-incline press at 30–45° (dumbbell or Smith) — upper pec, front delt.
2. Weighted pull-up or chin-up — lats, lower traps (pull-up) or biceps (chin-up).
3. Seated cable row, narrow neutral handle — lats; some mid-back.
4. Pec deck — whole pec, both heads.
5. Cable lateral raise, pulley at hand height — side delt.
6. Skull-crusher, EZ-bar, lowered behind the head — triceps long head.
7. EZ-bar preacher curl, full stretch — brachialis, brachioradialis, lower biceps.
8. Optional, if time: dumbbell shrug; wrist curl and reverse wrist curl.

**What doubles up, so you do not add redundant work**

- **Bench and incline press cover the front delt** (Lanza2024). No front raises. An overhead press is optional: keep
  it if you want the strength, not because the delts need it.
- **Bench press covers the lateral triceps head** (Brandao2020). The two extensions cover the long head. No pushdowns
  or kickbacks needed.
- **Pulldowns and chin-ups count toward biceps** (Gentil2015). **Rows count about half** (Mannarino2021). Two curls a
  week on top is enough variety: one with the arm behind you, one with the arm in front.
- **The preacher curl covers brachialis and brachioradialis** (Kobayashi2026). No separate hammer or reverse curls
  needed. Add them only if you enjoy them; they are untested.
- **Lateral raises and wide rows load the upper traps** on EMG. Shrugs are optional.
- **Rows and pull-ups load the rear delts**, but the EMG evidence conflicts, so the reverse pec deck stays in.
- **The bench does nothing useful for side delts** (Lanza2024). Lateral raises are not optional.

## New avenues

1. **Triceps: overhead extension vs skull-crusher vs dips in trained lifters.** Maeo2023 has not been repeated in
   trained people, and skull-crushers (to the forehead vs behind the head), dips, close-grip bench and kickbacks have
   never been tested against anything for growth. Re-check every few months.
2. **Hammer curls, reverse curls and wrist curls: any growth data at all?** Brachialis, brachioradialis and
   wrist-flexor size after these exercises is unmeasured. Check theses, conference abstracts and the climbing
   literature (finger-flexor hypertrophy with imaging, not tape).
3. **First back-hypertrophy trial.** Still none for lats, traps or rear delts. Searching SportRxiv through the
   Crossref prefix filter (`prefix:10.51224`) worked today; add it to the routine and re-run it quarterly.
4. **Upper pec in trained lifters, and flyes or cables vs presses.** Chaves2020 is still the only incline trial, in
   beginners, with an unknown bench angle. Cable crossovers (either direction) have no EMG or growth data at all.

## Doubts / audit flags

- **Grade counts.** 68 ranked rows: 20 grade A (counting ties and "A for one purpose"), 14 grade B, 33 grade C,
  1 grade D. Most "Skip or demote" items are outside the ranked tables. The auditor should recount.
- **46 of the 48 new CSV rows are ABSTRACT only** (publisher sites blocked). FULL: Paoli2016 (two paragraphs only).
  Exercise details for Sato2021, Havers2025 and Augustin2025 were read in Europe PMC full text; their numbers come
  from abstracts. Stasinaki2018, Muyor2022 and Padovan2025b are not in PubMed; their abstracts were read on Crossref.
- **Rank order inside grade C is my judgment**, not data. This applies to all of sections 3 (ranks 3–5), 4, 5, 8 and
  14, and to the lower rows of every table.
- **Setup details that do not come from the trials.** Chaves2020: incline angle unknown (full text HTTP 500 again).
  Kobayashi2026: bench angle unknown. Brandao2020: bar path unknown, no magnitudes, trial length or training status.
  Maeo2023: attachment and body position not in the abstract. The 30–45° incline, "behind the head" and "rope, facing
  away" instructions are standard setups chosen to match what each trial tested; they were not themselves tested.
- **Paoli2016 contradicts itself.** The abstract reports "whole muscle CSA of the lat" rising (p = 0.024). The results
  text gives the same p-value for "muscle area of the upper limb" (45.13 → 47.94 cm²). I rely only on the biopsy
  result (lat single-fibre area rose). This is the sole basis for grade B in the lat section. If the auditor judges it
  too thin, pulldown and seated row drop to C and the lat list is all grade C.
- **Corrections I could not read.** Kassiano2026b has a published correction (doi 10.1007/s00421-026-06144-6).
  Wadhi2022 has one (doi 10.1055/a-1643-7189). Both publishers are blocked. Neither is a retraction on Crossref.
- **Goto2019** (+48.7% triceps area in 8 weeks in trained men) is implausible and given no weight.
- **Conflicts between EMG studies.** Coratella2022 vs Padovan2026 on the side delt in behind-the-neck pressing (same
  lab). Franke2015 vs Rabello2024 on whether rows and pull-ups are enough for rear delts. Coratella2020 vs Rabello2024
  on thumbs-down lateral raises. Lusk2010 vs Buonsenso2025 on overhand vs underhand pulldowns.
- **Not used:** Kholinne 2018 (PMID 29503079, triceps head activity by shoulder angle). Its abstract's results and
  conclusion contradict each other. Not a CSV row.
- **Recalled, not searched for and given no weight:** fitness-industry EMG reports (for example the ones often
  quoted for kickbacks, concentration curls and "best" delt or triceps exercises). They are not in PubMed and I did
  not look for them.
- **"None found" means not found today**, with these searches, in PubMed, Europe PMC and Crossref: any growth or EMG
  study on face pulls, cable crossovers (high-to-low or low-to-high), lean-away or behind-the-body cable lateral
  raises, machine lateral raises, machine or cable pullovers, spider curls, JM presses, kickbacks (growth), wrist
  curls or reverse wrist curls; any growth study on hammer curls, reverse curls, dips, close-grip bench, shrugs,
  upright rows, overhead presses (delt size), pull-ups, pulldowns or rows (back size).
- **Small EMG samples carry much of the grade C ranking:** Fenwick2009 (n = 7), Marchetti2011 (n = 8), Coratella2022
  (n = 8), Coratella2020b / 2023a / 2023b (n = 10 each). Eight of the EMG rows come from one research group
  (Coratella, Padovan and colleagues). Their effect sizes (d up to 9.9) are within-subject EMG artefacts, not a
  measure of importance.
- **Other small flags.** Lehman2004: isometric holds; n taken from DiFonza2026. Youdas2010: values above 100% of the
  reference contraction, and it tested a commercial handle. Fenwick2009: PubMed holds a second record with the same
  title (PMID 19620925); not checked. Andersen2008: women with neck pain. Chomiuk2025: the abstract's hang-time SDs
  look misprinted. Gschneidner2025: muscle area was estimated, and its equivalence test did not pass the pre-set
  threshold. Larsen2025: equipment-company funding (carried over from yesterday).
- **Author overlap.** Schoenfeld is a co-author on Augustin2025, Pedrosa2023, Wolf2026, Schoenfeld2013 and several of
  yesterday's studies. Nippard (commercial fitness content) is a co-author on Augustin2025 and Wolf2025.
- **Barbalho-group trials:** none used. Steele and Fisher, co-authors on some of those papers, are authors of
  Gschneidner2025, which is a separate pre-registered multi-site trial.
- **Retraction check.** All 47 new DOIs were resolved on Crossref and checked for `update-to` / `updated-by`. Only the
  Kassiano2026b correction appeared. Oliveira2009 has no DOI and was checked on PubMed only.
- **Searches run:** about 70. PubMed E-utilities 44, Europe PMC 15, Crossref bibliographic 9 (5 of them restricted
  to SportRxiv), Semantic Scholar 3 (two returned HTTP 429, one returned nothing relevant). Plus about 50 Crossref DOI
  look-ups and 7 Europe PMC full-text fetches.
