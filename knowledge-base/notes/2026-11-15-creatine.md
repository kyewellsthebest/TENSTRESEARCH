# Creatine monohydrate — muscle, strength, cognition, kidneys, hair, water, loading, timing, non-responders

Run date: 2026-11-15 · Topic status: researched (needs audit) · Studies file: `2026-11-15-creatine-studies.csv` (20 rows)

**Evidence tags:** strength of evidence is given as strong / moderate / limited / very limited, followed by the citation key
from the CSV. Access is marked FULL (full text read), ABSTRACT or SECONDARY.

**A word on funding.** A lot of this literature comes from a small, connected group of authors: Candow, Forbes, Antonio
and the ISSN. Several of them advise creatine makers, including AlzChem/Creapure, Bear Balanced and Create, or have been
given creatine for free. I flag this on each study. The main findings below also hold up in meta-analyses by teams with no
declared conflicts: Desai2024 (UNSW), Wang2026, Tsiaras2026, deSouzaAlmeida2026 and Naeini2025. So the conflicts don't
change the overall picture, but they do matter on the hair-loss and timing questions.

---

## 1. Muscle mass / hypertrophy (longitudinal)

- **Creatine plus lifting adds about 1–1.4 kg of fat-free mass over a typical 6–12-week trial, compared with lifting plus
  placebo.** Strength: **strong**.
  - Ashtary-Larky2025 (61 trials; FULL): fat-free mass +1.39 kg (95% CI 1.07 to 1.70); body mass +0.89 kg (0.76 to 1.01);
    no change in fat mass.
  - Desai2024 (12 RCTs in adults under 50, pre-registered, no industry conflicts; ABSTRACT): lean body mass +1.14 kg
    (0.69 to 1.59); fat mass −0.73 kg (−1.34 to −0.11).
  - Wang2026 (three-level model; FULL): lean mass g = 0.30 (0.18 to 0.41).
- **Trained lifters respond at least as well as beginners, and possibly better.** Strength: **moderate**.
  - Ashtary-Larky2025 (FULL): trained +1.82 kg (1.10 to 2.55, 28 trials) vs untrained +1.23 kg (0.91 to 1.56, 26 trials).
    The difference between the two groups was not significant (d = 0.39, −0.13 to 0.91).
  - So the common idea that "it only works for beginners" is not supported.
- **Measured directly, the extra muscle growth is real but small.** Strength: **moderate**.
  - Burke2023 is the only meta-analysis that uses direct imaging (MRI, CT or ultrasound): 10 RCTs and 44 outcomes (FULL).
  - Pooled standardised effect 0.11 (95% credible interval −0.02 to 0.25). That is roughly 0.10–0.16 cm of extra muscle
    thickness. For example, elbow extensors +0.16 cm (−0.10 to 0.39) and elbow flexors +0.10 cm (−0.13 to 0.32).
  - **Only 2 of the 10 studies used trained lifters.**
- **Part of the "lean mass" gain is water.** Strength: **moderate**.
  - DXA and bioimpedance count water in muscle as lean mass, and creatine pulls water into the cell. Burke2023 and
    Antonio2021 discuss this.
  - The gap between "≈1.4 kg fat-free mass" and "≈0.1 cm muscle thickness" is the honest summary: some of it is real tissue
    and some of it is fluid.
- **Be sceptical of outlier estimates.**
  - Gu2026 reports fat-free mass +3.39 kg in young men. That is two to three times every other meta-analysis.
  - The paper already has a correction: two ineligible studies were removed, but the headline numbers were left
    unchanged (FULL for the correction).
  - Treat it as **very limited** and don't quote it.

## 2. Strength (longitudinal)

- **Creatine reliably adds to strength gains from lifting.** Strength: **strong**.
  - Wang2026 (63 studies, n = 1,489; FULL):
    - upper-body strength g = 0.54 (0.37 to 0.71), falling to g = 0.36 (0.21 to 0.51) after the trim-and-fill
      correction for publication bias;
    - lower-body strength g = 0.55 (0.38 to 0.71), falling to g = 0.35 after trim-and-fill.
  - Read it as a small-to-moderate effect, with some inflation from publication bias.
- **In kilograms:** about +1.4 kg on bench and +5.6 kg on squat beyond placebo, over typical study lengths.
  Strength: **moderate**.
  - Kazeminasab2025 (69 RCTs; FULL): bench/chest press WMD 1.43 kg (0.53 to 2.34); squat WMD 5.64 kg (3.87 to 7.40);
    leg press not significant (3.13 kg, p = 0.11).
  - Trained participants, bench/chest press: +1.84 kg (0.63 to 3.05). Untrained: +0.95 kg (−0.49 to 2.40).
- **Older meta-analyses agree.** Strength: **strong**.
  - Lanhers2017, upper limb (53 studies): bench press ES 0.27 (0.13 to 0.40); global upper-limb ES 0.32 (0.19 to 0.45)
    (ABSTRACT).
  - Lanhers2015, lower limb (60 studies): global ES 0.24 (0.13 to 0.35) (ABSTRACT).
- **Acute vs longitudinal.** The long-term trials above are the evidence that matters.
  - Acute effects are a separate question. Creatine raises phosphocreatine stores and helps repeated high-intensity bouts,
    which shows up as better Wingate power in Gu2026 and Kazeminasab2025.
  - The proposed mechanism for the long-term gains is that you do more work per session, and that drives the extra
    adaptation.

## 3. Water retention / scale weight

- **Expect about 1 kg on the scale, mostly in the first week, and more if you load.** Strength: **strong** for the weight
  gain; **moderate** for "it's mostly water early on".
  - Body mass rose 0.89 kg (0.76 to 1.01) in Ashtary-Larky2025.
  - Antonio2021 (narrative review; FULL; ISSN authors with industry links): water retention is the most common early
    effect, especially when loading. Training studies of 5–10 weeks mostly show no increase in total body water beyond
    what you'd expect from the extra muscle.
- **No meaningful increase in body fat.** Strength: **strong**.
  - Fat mass did not change in Ashtary-Larky2025 or Wang2026, and fell slightly in Desai2024.
- **It's not "bloat under the skin".** The fluid is mostly inside the muscle cells, so it won't blur visible abdominal
  definition. Strength: **limited** (based on mechanism and short studies discussed in Antonio2021).

## 4. Kidney safety

- **In healthy people, creatine raises the blood creatinine reading but does not harm the kidneys.** Strength: **strong**
  for healthy adults over periods of up to about 1–2 years.
  - Tsiaras2026 (19 RCTs plus 1 randomised crossover; ABSTRACT):
    - serum creatinine +0.13 mg/dL (0.07 to 0.18);
    - urea −0.60 mg/dL (−2.15 to 0.96);
    - eGFR −5.2 mL/min/1.73 m² (−15.0 to 4.6), not significant.
  - deSouzaAlmeida2026 (26 RCTs, n = 1,036; ABSTRACT):
    - creatinine +0.14 mg/dL (0.05 to 0.22);
    - **creatinine-based GFR −10.75 mL/min (−17.48 to −4.02)**;
    - **but GFR measured properly with ⁵¹Cr-EDTA clearance showed no fall (+5.89 mL/min, −0.30 to 12.08)**.
  - This is the key fact-check. Creatine breaks down to creatinine, so the standard kidney test reads worse even though the
    kidneys are filtering normally. It is a measurement artefact, not kidney damage.
- **Trained lifters on a high-protein diet show the same thing.** Strength: **limited** (single RCT, n = 26).
  - Lugaresi2013 (FULL): 12 weeks of creatine with protein intake of at least 1.2 g/kg/day. No change in ⁵¹Cr-EDTA GFR
    (group × time p = 0.64), albuminuria or proteinuria.
  - Kreider2003 (open-label, non-randomised, 21 months, n = 98 college football players; ABSTRACT): no clinically
    significant changes in a panel of 69 health markers.
- **Problems with the kidney evidence.**
  - Naeini2025 reports its pooled creatinine effect as "0.07 µmol/L". That is almost certainly a unit error (it is
    probably mg/dL); µmol/L would be a meaningless amount (FULL).
  - deSouzaEsilva2019 says in its abstract that creatinine "did not significantly alter", next to SMD 0.48 (0.24 to 0.73),
    p = .001. The paper contradicts itself.
  - Neither error changes the conclusion. Both are reasons not to quote those two papers' numbers.
- **What is not known.** Strength: **very limited**.
  - There are almost no RCTs longer than a year.
  - Data on people with existing kidney disease are few. Tsiaras2026 explicitly calls for longer trials.
- **The "creatine causes kidney damage" claim comes from isolated case reports, not trials.** Strength: **limited**
  (SECONDARY, via Antonio2021). I did not open the individual case reports.

## 5. Hair loss / DHT

- **Where the claim comes from.** vanderMerwe2009 (ABSTRACT, plus details quoted in Lak2025 [FULL]).
  - A single crossover study of 20 college rugby players: 7 days of loading at 25 g/day, then 14 days at 5 g/day.
  - DHT rose 56% after loading and stayed 40% above baseline; the DHT:testosterone ratio rose 36%.
  - **It never measured hair.**
  - The creatine condition started lower than placebo (DHT 0.98 vs 1.26 nmol/L), so regression to the mean is possible.
  - All values stayed inside the normal range (0.8–3.5 nmol/L).
  - It has never been replicated. A letter to the editor questioned it (Green 2010, doi 10.1097/JSM.0b013e3181df5cad).
  - Strength: **very limited**.
- **The direct test found no effect.** Strength: **limited** (one RCT).
  - Lak2025 (FULL): 12-week RCT in 45 resistance-trained men, of whom 38 finished; 5 g/day.
  - No group × time effect on total testosterone, free testosterone, DHT, DHT:T, hair density, follicular units or hair
    thickness (Trichogram and FotoFinder).
  - Caveats:
    - it used no loading phase, so it does not mirror vanderMerwe2009;
    - it was small and short;
    - **it has conflicts of interest**: Antonio advises Creapure, Bear Balanced and Create; Forbes advises Bear Balanced
      and received Creapure creatine; Tinsley consults for supplement firms; and the paper thanks a BPI Sports
      distributor.
- **Bottom line on hair.** There is no good evidence that creatine causes hair loss, and the only direct test was null.
  The evidence base is thin on both sides. If you are already losing hair, creatine is very unlikely to be the deciding
  factor, but nobody has proved that over years.

## 6. Cognition

- **Healthy young adults: little or no reliable benefit.** Strength: **limited**.
  - Prokopidis2023 (8 RCTs; ABSTRACT): memory SMD 0.29 (0.04 to 0.53) overall, but **young adults SMD 0.03 (−0.14 to
    0.20)**. Older adults (66–76 years): SMD 0.88 (0.22 to 1.55).
  - A letter (Eckert 2023, doi 10.1093/nutrit/nuac108) showed that outcomes were double-counted. After re-analysis, the
    overall effect was reportedly no longer significant except in older adults (SECONDARY, via Citherlet2026).
- **Xu2024 has the same flaw.** Strength: **limited**.
  - 16 RCTs, n = 492 (ABSTRACT): memory SMD 0.31 (0.18 to 0.44); processing speed SMD −0.51 (−1.01 to −0.01).
  - It has a corrigendum about a translation error in the attention section.
  - A 2026 commentary (Citherlet2026; FULL) shows the same unit-of-analysis error: multiple subtests from the same
    participants were counted as independent.
  - Citherlet reports that EFSA's 2024 opinion said no conclusions could be drawn from it. That is SECONDARY; I did not
    open the EFSA opinion.
- **Possible benefit under stress** (sleep deprivation, older age, vegetarians). This is plausible and of interest, but
  outside this run's scope. See New avenues.

## 7. Loading vs no loading

- **Both routes fill the muscle to the same level. Loading just gets there faster.** Strength: **strong** for the
  physiology (Hultman1996, n = 31, muscle biopsies; ABSTRACT).
  - 20 g/day for 6 days raised muscle total creatine by about 20%, and 2 g/day kept it there.
  - 3 g/day reached the same roughly 20% rise in about 28 days.
  - After stopping, levels took about 30 days to return to baseline.
- **Loading doesn't change the long-term outcome.** Strength: **moderate**.
  - In Ashtary-Larky2025's subgroups, maintenance-only protocols gave fat-free mass +1.50 kg (1.11 to 1.90). Protocols
    with loading plus long maintenance gave +1.36 kg (0.60 to 2.12) (FULL, Table).
  - Wang2026 found no moderating effect of dosing protocol.
- **Cost of loading:** more early water weight and a higher chance of stomach upset. Strength: **limited**.

## 8. Timing (before vs after training)

- **Timing doesn't matter. Taking it consistently does.** Strength: **moderate** (several small RCTs, all null on the main
  outcomes).
  - Dinan2022 (FULL): 34 resistance-trained athletes, 8 weeks, randomised, double-blind and placebo-controlled; 5 g taken
    before vs after training. No difference between groups in any outcome. Academic funding.
  - Forbes2021 (ABSTRACT): within-subject unilateral design, n = 10, 8 weeks, with ultrasound muscle thickness. No
    difference between before and after. Authors have industry links.
- **The "post-workout is better" claim comes from one weak study.**
  - Antonio2013 (FULL): 19 recreational bodybuilders, 4 weeks, no placebo arm. No significant interaction.
  - The "post is possibly better" claim rests only on magnitude-based inference, a method since discredited.
  - The creatine was supplied by VPX Sports, and Antonio was a former VPX consultant.
  - Strength: **very limited**.

## 9. Non-responders

- **Some people take up little creatine into muscle.** Strength: **very limited** for the size of this group.
  - Syrotuik2004 (ABSTRACT): n = 11, 5-day load, biopsies. 3 responders, 5 quasi-responders and
    3 non-responders.
  - Non-responders had higher starting muscle creatine, fewer type II fibres and smaller fibres, and showed no gain in
    1RM.
  - The often-quoted "20–30% of people are non-responders" figure rests on very small biopsy samples like this one. Treat
    it as a rough idea, not a measured rate.
- **What predicts a big response:** low starting stores (for example, little meat or fish in the diet) and more type II
  muscle fibre. Strength: **limited**.
  - Heavy meat eaters already start nearer the ceiling, so they may get less.
  - There is no cheap test for whether you respond. A muscle biopsy or MR spectroscopy is the only direct measure.
  - In practice, judge it over 4–8 weeks by scale weight and performance. That is noisy, though.

---

## Verdict

Creatine monohydrate is the best-evidenced legal supplement for lifters.

- **Muscle:** expect about **+1–1.5 kg of fat-free mass** over a training block, beyond what training alone gives.
  Measured directly, the gain in muscle size is **small: about 0.1–0.16 cm more muscle thickness**. Some of the
  scale-weight gain is water inside the muscle.
- **Strength:** expect about **+1–2 kg on bench and around +5 kg on squat** beyond placebo. That is a small-to-moderate
  effect, g ≈ 0.35–0.55 depending on how you correct for publication bias.
- **Who it works for:** trained lifters respond at least as well as beginners.
- **Kidneys:** it doesn't damage healthy kidneys. It does make the standard creatinine and eGFR blood test read worse.
- **Hair:** the hair-loss story rests on one unreplicated 2009 study that never measured hair, and the one direct test
  was null.
- **Cognition:** claims for healthy young adults are overstated because of statistical double-counting.
- **Loading and timing:** loading is optional, and timing doesn't matter.
- **Non-responders:** they exist, but how common they are is unknown.

## Practical recommendation for Kye

1. **Take 5 g of creatine monohydrate every day, including rest days, at whatever time you'll remember.**
   - At 87 kg, 3 g/day would also work in the long run. 5 g is cheap insurance and is the dose most trials used.
   - Don't bother with pre- vs post-workout timing.
   - Don't pay more for HCl, buffered (Kre-Alkalyn) or "creatine blends". Monohydrate is what was tested.
2. **Loading is optional.**
   - If you want the effect within a week, take 20 g/day as 4 × 5 g with meals for 5–7 days, then 5 g/day.
   - Otherwise, just start at 5 g/day and you'll be saturated in about 3–4 weeks.
   - Loading means more early water weight and a higher chance of stomach upset.
3. **Expect the scale to go up by about 1 kg in the first weeks.** That isn't fat.
   - If you're cutting, recalibrate your weight trend for this rather than cutting calories further.
   - At 13.6% body fat, you won't look "softer".
4. **Before any blood test, tell your GP you take creatine.**
   - Your creatinine will read higher and your eGFR lower, which can trigger a false kidney alarm.
   - If they need a real number, ask for a cystatin C–based eGFR, or stop creatine for about 4 weeks before the test.
   - If you have known kidney disease or diabetes, talk to your doctor before taking it.
5. **Be realistic about how much it does.**
   - It's a small edge for a lifter who already trains 1.5 h a day: about a kilo of lean mass and a few kilos on your
     lifts over months.
   - It won't fix poor training, sleep or protein.
   - It is one of the few supplements worth the money.
6. **Australia.** Creatine monohydrate is legal and not on the WADA list.
   - If you ever compete in a tested sport, buy a batch-tested product (Informed Sport or HASTA) to reduce contamination
     risk. A separate topic in the queue covers contamination and testing.
7. **If you see no change in scale weight or performance after 6–8 weeks**, you may be a low responder. Creatine is cheap
   and harmless, so it's reasonable to keep taking it anyway, but don't expect much more from it.

## New avenues

1. **Creatine during a calorie deficit.** Does it preserve lean mass or strength better than placebo when trained lifters
   cut? This is relevant to Kye's future cuts.
2. **Creatine and cognition under sleep deprivation or heavy training load.** Do the acute high-dose studies on sleep
   loss hold up? (Not searched this run.) Is there any properly analysed meta-analysis
   for healthy adults? Does EFSA's 2024 opinion say anything more specific?
3. **Cystatin C vs creatinine-based eGFR in lifters.** How should a heavily muscled, high-protein, creatine-taking lifter
   read routine kidney blood tests? What does the evidence on cystatin C accuracy in athletes say?
4. **Responder biology.** Are there newer MR-spectroscopy studies on how often people respond poorly to creatine, and on
   whether diet (habitual meat intake) or genetics (SLC6A8 variants; see Ostojic 2026, Lifestyle Genomics) predict the
   response in trained lifters?

## Doubts / audit flags

- Several key numbers come from abstracts only: Desai2024, Tsiaras2026, deSouzaAlmeida2026, Lanhers2015/2017, Hultman1996,
  Prokopidis2023 and Xu2024. The auditor should open the full texts of Tsiaras2026 and deSouzaAlmeida2026 if possible.
- The meta-analyses overlap heavily in the trials they include. Agreement between them is not independent replication.
- The EFSA 2024 opinion and the Prokopidis re-analysis are SECONDARY, both via Citherlet2026.
- About 25 searches were used: PubMed E-utilities, Europe PMC and PMC full text. All DOIs were resolved via Crossref, and
  corrections were checked through Crossref `updated-by`. No retractions were found among the cited papers.
