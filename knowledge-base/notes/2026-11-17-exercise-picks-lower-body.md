# Exercise picks, lower body and trunk — named exercises in rank order, with the evidence grade behind each

Run date: 2026-11-17 · Topic status: researched (needs audit) · Studies file:
`2026-11-17-exercise-picks-lower-body-studies.csv` (59 new rows; older studies are cited by their keys in
`knowledge-base/STUDIES.csv`)

This note names exercises. It builds on `2026-11-16-exercise-selection-lower-body.md`, which answered "what does the
research show" but named too few lifts. Chest, back, shoulders and arms are in the companion upper-body note.

## How to read the grades

- **A — won or tied a head-to-head training study.** A study lasting weeks compared this exercise with another and
  measured muscle size. The row says what it beat or tied.
- **B — shown to grow the muscle, or backed by a tested principle in this muscle.** Either a training study measured
  growth from this exercise with no head-to-head win, or the exercise applies something that has trial support in the
  same muscle (for example, a straight knee for the gastrocnemius).
- **C — mechanism or EMG only.** Anatomy, joint action or muscle-activity readings. No growth data. An EMG difference
  is not proof of more growth: in Plotkin2023 the hip thrust had higher glute EMG and grew the glutes no more.
- **D — popular but shown inferior, or redundant.** These are in "Skip or demote".

**Three blunt warnings.**

- **Grade A does not mean "proven for you".** Almost every head-to-head trial used beginners for 6–12 weeks with 8–20
  people per group. Trained-lifter data exist for only a few lifts: leg press (Larsen2025c), barbell and machine squat
  (HernandezBelmonte2023), Bulgarian split squat (Wang2026c), calf raises (Larsen2025b), lying leg curl (Cadeo2023)
  and two mixed leg programmes (Hermann2026, Plotkin2026).
- **A tie in a small trial means "too small to tell", not "equal".**
- **Where two exercises have the same grade and no trial compares them, I say "no data separates these".** The order
  then comes from practical things: how heavy you can load it, stability, fatigue cost and time.

## 1. Quadriceps

Squat-type lifts grow the three vasti (lateralis, medialis, intermedius). They barely grow the rectus femoris. Leg
extensions grow the rectus femoris. So the quads get two lists.

### 1a. Vasti (outer, inner and deep quad)

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Barbell back squat, high-bar, hip crease below the knee** | The most-tested quad lift. Beat the hip thrust for quad size by 3.6 cm² (95% CI 0.7 to 6.4). A deep squat (120° knee bend) grew the front thigh more than a shallow one (60°); the abstract gives "4–7%" without saying whether that is the gain or the gap. Barbell and machine squat grew the quads equally in trained men (within 2.0 points). Also grows glutes and adductors (sections 3, 4). | A | Plotkin2023 (FULL); Bloomquist2013 (ABSTRACT); Kubo2019 (ABSTRACT); HernandezBelmonte2023 (ABSTRACT) |
| 2 | **Smith machine squat, same depth** | Beat the leg extension at the lower outer quad (+18.2% vs +11.2%). Same growth as the free-bar squat in one 8-week trial. More stable, so the legs fail before balance does. | A | Kassiano2026 (ABSTRACT); MarcosFrutos2025 (ABSTRACT) |
| 3 | **Leg press (45° or seated), feet hip-width, at least 90–100° of knee bend** | Tied the leg extension for the vasti (+4.4–6.2% vs +5.0–7.2%) and, unlike it, grew the glute max +15.4% and adductor magnus +6.2%. In lifters with 7 years of training it grew the quads 1.1–1.9 mm in 8 weeks, and going deeper than 100° added nothing for the quads. Foot width and toe angle made no EMG difference. | A | Kinoshita2026 (FULL); Larsen2025c (ABSTRACT); MartinFuentes2022 (ABSTRACT) |
| 4 | **Leg extension** | Tied the leg press for the vasti; lost to the Smith squat at the lower outer quad. You need it anyway for the rectus femoris (1b). Use the bottom of the rep: top-half partials grew no more than doing nothing at two of four sites. | A | Kinoshita2026 (FULL); Kassiano2026 (ABSTRACT); Pedrosa2022 (ABSTRACT) |
| 5 | **Hack squat** | Never tested on its own. It only appears inside a three-lift rotation that grew the thighs the same as leg press alone. It is a squat on rails, and machines grow muscle as well as free weights. | B | Kassiano2025b (ABSTRACT); Haugen2023b (FULL) |
| 6 | **Bulgarian split squat (rear foot on a bench, dumbbells or Smith)** | Grew the outer quad +9.3% in 6 weeks in trained men, with no comparison exercise. Matched the back squat for strength in rugby players. Costs twice the time. | B | Wang2026c (ABSTRACT); Speirs2016 (ABSTRACT) |

**No data separates ranks 1–3.** They are in that order because the barbell squat has the most trials behind it. If
you like the hack squat best, use it: rank 5 means "untested", not "worse".

**Also fine**

- **Forward or walking lunge** — tied the incline leg press for outer quad and rectus femoris growth, but in 8 women
  (Amanuma2025, ABSTRACT). A longer step raised quad, glute and adductor EMG (Escamilla2025, ABSTRACT).
- **Front squat** — no growth study. EMG in 10 bodybuilders showed more rectus femoris activity than the full back
  squat (Coratella2021, ABSTRACT). Grade C.
- **Pendulum squat, belt squat, goblet squat, heel-elevated squat** — no training study found for any of them. Grade
  C. They are squat patterns, so they probably behave like ranks 1–5.
- **Step-up** — only tested inside a squat-plus-step-up programme, where the inner quad grew 4.1% in trained lifters
  (Bartolomei2025, ABSTRACT). Grade C alone.
- **Low-bar or wide-stance squat** — a high-bar narrow stance put more of the load on the knee and gave higher outer
  quad EMG (Larsen2021, ABSTRACT). No growth data. Squat the way that lets you go deep without pain.

**Skip or demote**

- **Quarter squats and very shallow squats** — D. A 60° squat lost to a 120° squat for front-thigh size
  (Bloomquist2013). A 90° half squat matched the full squat for quads but lost badly on glutes and adductors
  (Kubo2019).
- **Top-half leg extensions (short, squeezed reps)** — D. Lost to bottom-half reps in both trials and to full reps in
  the second (Pedrosa2022, Pedrosa2025).
- **Rotating squat, hack squat and leg press through the week** — D, redundant. The rotation grew no more than leg
  press alone in 70 women (Kassiano2025b). Pick one or two and progress them.
- **Bottom-half-only ("lengthened partial") leg presses and leg extensions as an upgrade** — no gain shown in
  trained men: −1.4 cm² (95% CI −6.1 to 3.8) against full reps (Plotkin2026, ABSTRACT). Harmless, not better.

**Evidence notes**

- Kinoshita2026 (FULL): 17 untrained adults; one leg did leg extensions, the other single-leg leg presses; 12 weeks,
  5 × 10 at 70% 1RM, twice a week; MRI of 17 muscles, assessors blinded. Whole quads +7.1% vs +4.9% (P ≥ 0.319). The
  leg press only went to 90° of knee bend. Funded by a Yamaha Motor Foundation for Sports grant.
- Larsen2025c (ABSTRACT): 23 trained lifters (7.2 ± 3.5 years); each leg one condition; 8 weeks, 4 sets to failure
  twice a week; leg press to 100° vs as deep as possible (154 ± 8°). Bayes factors 0.14–0.22 in favour of no
  difference. It did not measure glutes or adductors.
- Bloomquist2013 (ABSTRACT): 17 male students (8 deep, 9 shallow); 12 weeks. The deep group gained about 20% 1RM in
  both squats; the shallow group gained 36% in the shallow squat and 9% in the deep one.
- Pedrosa2022 (ABSTRACT; 45 untrained women) and Pedrosa2025 (ABSTRACT; 50 untrained women): leg extension over
  100–65° of knee bend (bottom), 65–30° (top), or the full range. In Pedrosa2022 bottom-range reps beat full-range
  and top-range reps at three of four sites. In Pedrosa2025 bottom-range, full-range and mixed reps grew the same
  (13.2–17%) and all beat top-range reps. No intervals in either abstract.
- Plotkin2026 (ABSTRACT, ahead of print): 16 trained men (8.0 ± 4.9 years); one leg lengthened partials, the other
  full range, on leg press, leg extension and lying leg curl; 8 weeks. Outer quad area rose 9.3 cm² (95% CI 6.8 to
  11.8) with no difference between legs. Gschneidner2025 (ABSTRACT; 297 people, 15 sites, 12 weeks) agrees, but it
  estimated muscle area instead of imaging it, and its authors work for commercial gym companies.
- Wang2026c (ABSTRACT): 24 trained men; 6 weeks; 80% 1RM Bulgarian split squats on one leg, light blood-flow-
  restricted ones on the other. Outer quad thickness +9.3% and +8.1%. A 9% gain in 6 weeks in trained men is high.
- Hermann2026 (FULL): 41 trained lifters did leg extension, Smith squat, seated leg curl and Romanian deadlift for 8
  weeks. In the normal-order group the front thigh grew 4.6–6.2% and the outer thigh 3.5–4.4%. This is not an
  exercise comparison. It shows that this set of four works in trained people. Funded by a fitness company whose
  owner is a co-author.
- Earp2015 (ABSTRACT; 36 untrained men; 8 weeks) and Chiu2025 (ABSTRACT; 11 novice women; 6 weeks): squats grew the
  vasti in both.

### 1b. Rectus femoris (the middle-front quad that also crosses the hip)

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Leg extension, leaning back** (seat back reclined so the hip is at about 40° of flexion; if the seat is fixed, slide your hips forward and lie back) | Beat the upright version for rectus femoris growth (Bayes factor > 100 at both sites). The leg extension itself beat the Smith squat (+11–18% vs +2–8%) and the leg press (+13.2% vs +1.1%). | A | Larsen2025a (ABSTRACT); Kassiano2026 (ABSTRACT); Kinoshita2026 (FULL) |
| 2 | **Leg extension, upright seat** | Same wins over squat and leg press. Lost to the lean-back version. | A | Kassiano2026 (ABSTRACT); Kinoshita2026 (FULL) |
| 3 | **Reverse Nordic** (kneel, lean back with a straight line from knee to shoulder) | Rectus femoris thickness and area rose after 8 weeks (d = 2.2–3.1), with no control group. The hip stays straight, so the muscle is stretched. EMG was no higher than bodyweight squats. Hard to load. | B | AlonsoFernandez2019 (ABSTRACT); Pereira2024 (ABSTRACT) |
| 4 | **Cable or machine hip flexion** (or hanging leg raise) | Four weeks of isometric hip-flexion training thickened the rectus femoris in untrained men. Isometric only; no gym version tested. | B | Ema2018 (ABSTRACT) |
| 5 | **Sissy squat** | No study found. Same idea as the reverse Nordic: knee bent, hip straight. | C | none found |

**Also fine**

- **Lunge** — in 8 women the rectus femoris grew as much as with the leg press (Amanuma2025). Too small to lean on.
- **Long-length isometric holds on the leg extension** — in 23 trained lifters, holds near the bottom matched full
  reps, with a hint of more growth high on the front thigh: −0.11 cm (95% HDI −0.24 to 0.02) (Varovic2025b,
  ABSTRACT). Uncertain.

**Skip or demote**

- **Squat, leg press and hack squat as rectus femoris builders** — D. Leg press +1.1% (Kinoshita2026); squat 0%
  (Kubo2019), none at any site (Earp2015), +2–8% (Kassiano2026).

**Evidence notes**

- Larsen2025a (ABSTRACT): 22 untrained men; each leg one hip angle; 10 weeks. No difference for the outer quad
  (Bayes factor 0.07). The abstract gives no size in millimetres.
- AlonsoFernandez2019 (ABSTRACT): 26 people; 8 weeks plus 4 weeks off. Effect sizes above 2 in 8 weeks are
  implausibly large, and everything returned to baseline in 4 weeks. Treat it as "it probably grows the muscle",
  nothing more.

## 2. Hamstrings

Four muscles. Three cross the hip and the knee: biceps femoris long head, semitendinosus and semimembranosus. The
biceps femoris short head crosses only the knee, so only curls train it.

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seated leg curl** (hips flexed, torso upright or leaning forward, let the knee go fully straight) | Beat the lying leg curl: whole hamstrings +14.1% vs +9.3%; long head +14.4% vs +6.5%. A stretched seated curl beat the Nordic: +18% vs +11%. **Covers:** long head, semitendinosus, semimembranosus; short head as well as any curl. | A | Maeo2021 (FULL); Maeo2024 (FULL) |
| 2 | **45° back extension done as a hip hinge** (pad below the hips, back flat, knees straight, hold a plate or barbell; single-leg in the trials) | Beat the Nordic for long-head growth (d = 1.03, p = 0.037). In a second trial the long head grew +13.3% and the semimembranosus +12.5% in 6 weeks. Higher biceps femoris EMG than the Romanian deadlift. **Covers:** long head, semimembranosus. | A | Bourne2017 (ABSTRACT); Carmichael2022 (ABSTRACT); Andersen2021 (ABSTRACT) |
| 3 | **Romanian deadlift / stiff-leg deadlift** (barbell, soft knees, lower until the hamstrings stop you, not until the plates touch) | Tied the Nordic for whole-hamstring size twice (+7.0% vs +11.4%, unresolved; and P = 0.60). Grows the semimembranosus (+11.2%; +5.7–7.4% from bottom-half eccentrics). Loads far heavier than rank 2 and trains glutes and lower back too. **Covers:** semimembranosus best. | A | Morin2025 (ABSTRACT); Crawford2025 (ABSTRACT); Kawama2024 (ABSTRACT) |
| 4 | **Nordic curl** | Tied the deadlift variants overall. Best for the two muscles the hinges miss: semitendinosus +24–26% and short head +22%. Lost to the seated curl overall and to the back extension for the long head (+5–9%). **Covers:** semitendinosus, short head. | A | Morin2025 (ABSTRACT); Maeo2024 (FULL); Pai2025 (ABSTRACT) |
| 5 | **Glute-ham raise** | No study found. It is a Nordic with a hip hinge added, so it should behave like ranks 2 and 4 combined. | C | none found |

**No trial compares rank 2 with rank 3, or either with the seated curl.** Rank 2 is above rank 3 only because it has
a head-to-head win and long-head growth data; the Romanian deadlift has only ties.

**Also fine**

- **Romanian vs stiff-leg deadlift** — no data separates these. Pick one.
- **Good morning, cable pull-through** — no training study found. Same hinge as rank 3. Grade C.
- **Standing single-leg curl** — no study found. The hip is straight, as in the lying curl, so expect lying-curl
  results. Grade C.
- **Toes pointed or pulled up on a curl** — makes no difference: 10 weeks in trained adults, p = 0.596 (Cadeo2023,
  ABSTRACT).

**Skip or demote**

- **Lying (prone) leg curl** — D if a seated machine is free. It still grew the hamstrings +9.3% and trains the short
  head as well as the seated curl (P = 0.190), so use it when the seated machine is taken (Maeo2021).
- **Squat, leg press, hip thrust as hamstring work** — D. Hamstrings unchanged after squats (Kubo2019), "little to no
  growth" after squats or hip thrusts (Plotkin2023), +3.2% after leg press (Kinoshita2026).
- **Conventional deadlift for hamstrings** — C. In EMG reviews the deadlift works the erectors and quads harder than
  the hamstrings; the Romanian deadlift reverses that (MartinFuentes2020, ABSTRACT). No growth study.
- **Razor curl** — negligible change in long-head fascicle length in 6 weeks; no size data (Pollard2019, ABSTRACT).

**Evidence notes**

- **What the "hip extension exercise" was.** Bourne2017 and Carmichael2022 call it only "hip extension" in their
  abstracts. A later trial's full text says earlier studies from this group used a 45° hip extension bench with one
  ankle secured (MartinSanAgustin2025). So "45° back extension" is SECONDARY for both.
- Bourne2017 (ABSTRACT): 30 recreationally active men, 10 per group; 10 weeks; MRI. Semitendinosus growth did not
  differ between the two exercises (d = 0.69, p = 0.239). Its correction changed the competing-interests statement.
  Lazarczuk2024 (ABSTRACT) has the same design and numbers and is very likely the same trial re-analysed.
- Carmichael2022 (ABSTRACT): 24 recreationally active men, 12 per group; 6 weeks; MRI. The eccentric version grew
  the long head and semimembranosus; an isometric version grew only the semitendinosus (+15%, P = 0.054).
- Kawama2024 (ABSTRACT): 36 young men; 10 weeks; eccentric-only stiff-leg deadlifts in the bottom half of the range,
  5 seconds per rep. Only the semimembranosus grew.
- Pai2025 (ABSTRACT; 11 people; 9 weeks; MRI): Nordic curls grew the short head +22%, semitendinosus +26%, long head
  +9%, semimembranosus +6%. Andrews2025 (same project, 12 people): long head +8%.
- Frouin2024 (ABSTRACT): 45 people in 3 groups; 9 weeks of seated leg curl plus stiff-leg deadlift. Semitendinosus
  +26.5 ± 25.5%; the long head and semimembranosus did not differ from control (p = 1.0 and 0.178). That does not
  fit Maeo2021. The spread between people was huge.
- Morin2026 (ABSTRACT, ahead of print; a modelling paper on the Morin2025 data, so not new evidence): in curls and
  Nordics the hamstring working at the longest length and highest force grew most. That rule did not explain why the
  stiff-leg deadlift grew the semimembranosus.
- Sahinis2025 (ABSTRACT; EMG meta-analysis, 619 participants): semitendinosus more active in the Nordic (SMD −0.33);
  biceps femoris more active in lying curls (SMD 0.94) and back extensions (SMD 0.34). The EMG pattern matches the
  growth pattern for the Nordic and the back extension.
- Hermann2026 (FULL): in trained lifters, seated curl plus Romanian deadlift grew the inner hamstrings 8.9–9.8% in 8
  weeks. No comparison exercise.

## 3. Glutes

### 3a. Gluteus maximus (lower and middle)

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Barbell back squat, below parallel** | Tied the hip thrust at every glute region. Full depth beat a 90° half squat three to one: +6.7% vs +2.2% (p = 0.008). Grows quads and adductors in the same set. | A | Plotkin2023 (FULL); Kubo2019 (ABSTRACT) |
| 2 | **Barbell hip thrust** (shoulders on a bench, full lockout) | Tied the deep squat: lower glute −1.6 cm² (95% CI −6.1 to 2.0), middle −0.5 (−4.0 to 2.6), upper −0.5 (−5.8 to 4.1). Little quad or lower-back fatigue, but it grows nothing else. | A | Plotkin2023 (FULL) |
| 3 | **Leg press** | Grew the glute max +15.4% in 12 weeks against 0% for the leg extension, even at only 90° of knee bend. Leg press plus stiff-leg deadlift grew the glutes 6% in 10 weeks. | A | Kinoshita2026 (FULL); Kassiano2023a (ABSTRACT) |
| 4 | **Cable kickback** (standing single-leg cable hip extension, torso braced) | Beat cable hip abduction for the whole glute max (+9% vs +5%) and most of all low down (+14% at the lowest site). Light loads; one leg at a time. | A | Attarieh2026 (ABSTRACT) |
| 5 | **Romanian deadlift or 45° back extension** | Both gave 94–140% more glute EMG than a seated back-extension machine. No study isolates either for glute size. You are already doing one for hamstrings. | C | Andersen2021 (ABSTRACT) |
| 6 | **Walking lunge with a long step, Bulgarian split squat, step-up** | High glute EMG (above 60% of maximum) in a review of 16 studies; step-ups highest. No study isolates any of them for glute size. | C | Neto2020 (ABSTRACT); Escamilla2025 (ABSTRACT) |

**No data separates ranks 1–3.** If you squat deep or leg press hard, your glute max is already trained. The hip
thrust is a choice, not a requirement.

**Also fine**

- **Barbell glute bridge (shoulders on the floor)** — higher glute EMG than the hip thrust in 10 men (Kennedy2024,
  ABSTRACT). No growth data. Grade C. Not a reason to swap.
- **Conventional, sumo or hex-bar deadlift** — EMG only. The hip thrust gave 16% more glute EMG than the hex-bar
  deadlift (Andersen2018, ABSTRACT). Grade C.
- **Reverse hyper** — only tested inside a hip-thrust-plus-reverse-hyper programme that matched a squat-plus-step-up
  programme in 19 trained lifters over 6 weeks (Bartolomei2025, ABSTRACT). Grade C alone.

**Skip or demote**

- **Half squats for glutes** — D (Kubo2019).
- **Adding hip thrusts on top of deep squats and leg press "for glutes"** — redundant unless you want more glute
  volume without more leg fatigue. The one trial that added them also added sets (Kassiano2023a).
- **Seated back-extension machine for glutes** — lowest glute EMG of three hip-extension lifts (Andersen2021). EMG
  only, so this is a demotion, not a verdict.

**Evidence notes**

- Plotkin2023 (FULL): 34 untrained adults; 9 weeks; MRI. Two co-authors sell hip-thrust products; a 2026 correction
  added that the hip thrust machine was donated and the trial was registered after the fact. No data changed.
- Kinoshita2026 (FULL): the gluteus medius (+4.9%, not significant; my calculation from the table means) and minimus
  did not grow with the leg press.
- Attarieh2026 (ABSTRACT): 17 adults; one leg cable hip extension, the other cable hip abduction; 10 weeks; MRI.
- Chiu2025 (ABSTRACT): 11 novice women; 6 weeks of squats; no glute growth, and a small loss in one group. It
  conflicts with Kubo2019 and Plotkin2023 and is too small and short to change the ranking.
- The two Barbalho trials in trained women (squat beat hip thrust) get no weight. See yesterday's note.

### 3b. Upper glute max, gluteus medius and minimus (the upper-outer hip)

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Standing cable hip abduction** (cuff at the ankle, working leg away from the stack) | Beat cable hip extension for the upper glute max (+10% vs +5% at the top site) and grew the gluteus medius and minimus +4% vs +2%. | A | Attarieh2026 (ABSTRACT) |
| 2 | **Seated hip abduction machine** | Same joint action, far easier to load and progress. Gluteus medius EMG equal to side-lying abduction, with less work done by the hip-flexor-side muscle (TFL). No growth study. | B | Attarieh2026 (ABSTRACT); deAlmeidaPaz2022 (ABSTRACT) |
| 3 | **Side-lying hip abduction** (ankle weight or low cable) | Gluteus medius at 70% of maximum EMG. Hard to load past beginner level. | C | McBeth2012 (ABSTRACT) |
| 4 | **Lateral band walk** | No record retrieved for it today. Anatomy only. | C | none found |

**Also fine**

- **Leaning forward on the abduction machine** — no study found. Do it if you feel it more; nobody has measured it.

**Skip or demote**

- **Clamshell** — gluteus medius at 33% of maximum, less than the hip flexors (McBeth2012). EMG only.
- **Counting squats, leg press or kickbacks as upper-glute work** — the leg press did not grow the gluteus medius
  (Kinoshita2026), and hip extension grew the upper glute only +5% (Attarieh2026).

**Evidence notes**

- Attarieh2026 is the only growth trial here. The abstract does not say whether the +4% vs +2% difference in the
  medius and minimus was significant. Rank 2's grade rests on it: the machine was not tested.
- deAlmeidaPaz2022 (ABSTRACT): 11 men, 10RM loads. Ebert2017 (ABSTRACT; review of 20 EMG studies): hip abduction
  and single-leg stance exercises load the gluteus medius most; bridges and quadruped exercises least.

## 4. Adductors

Squats and leg presses grow the adductor magnus, the big one at the back of the inner thigh that helps extend the
hip. They do not grow the adductor longus or brevis at the front. Those need direct adduction.

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Barbell back squat, below parallel** | Adductor volume +6.2% vs +2.7% for the half squat (p = 0.026). Beat the hip thrust by 2.5 cm² (95% CI 1.2 to 3.9). | A | Kubo2019 (ABSTRACT); Plotkin2023 (FULL) |
| 2 | **Leg press** | Adductor magnus +6.2% vs −0.9% for the leg extension. Adductor longus and brevis: no change at all. | A | Kinoshita2026 (FULL) |
| 3 | **Copenhagen adduction** (side plank, top leg on a bench; long lever = bench under the ankle) | Adductor longus thickness +18% in 8 weeks against a do-nothing control. Top tier for every adductor in a force-modelling study. Bodyweight, so loading runs out. | B | AlonsoFernandez2022 (ABSTRACT); Collings2026 (ABSTRACT) |
| 4 | **Seated hip adduction machine** | No growth study exists. It trains the same action as rank 3, through a longer stretch, with a weight stack. | C | none found |
| 5 | **Sumo deadlift or wide-stance squat** | Modelled adductor magnus forces were top tier in the deadlift and sumo deadlift. Wide squats raised adductor longus EMG in bodybuilders but ranked last of six rehab exercises in students. Mixed, and no growth data. | C | Collings2026 (ABSTRACT); Coratella2021 (ABSTRACT); Delmore2014 (ABSTRACT) |

**Judgment call, no data:** if you want direct adductor work, I would use the machine (rank 4) over the Copenhagen
(rank 3) because you can add weight every week. The grade order above is the evidence order, not my preference.

**Also fine**

- **Side-lying hip adduction, ball squeeze** — highest adductor longus EMG among rehab exercises (Delmore2014).
  Rehab loads. Grade C.

**Skip or demote**

- **Half squats and hip thrusts for adductors** — D (Kubo2019, Plotkin2023).
- **A leg day of only single-joint machines** — the adductor magnus shrank 1.5 ± 3.1% in novices who did only
  single-joint training for 10 weeks (VanVossel2024, ABSTRACT). Small, and close to measurement error.

**Evidence notes**

- AlonsoFernandez2022 (ABSTRACT, full text consulted): 45 active adults; one thickness site on the adductor longus;
  d = 1.8; back to baseline after 4 weeks off. Same lab and same too-clean pattern as AlonsoFernandez2019. A second,
  tiny trial in 12 teenage footballers found no extra thickness over normal training (AlonsoCalvete2021, ABSTRACT).
- Collings2026 (ABSTRACT): 15 people; forces estimated by a model, not measured growth. It did not test the machine.

## 5. Calves

### 5a. Gastrocnemius (the visible upper calf, inner and outer heads)

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Standing calf raise, knee locked straight** (machine or Smith, ball of the foot on a step, sink to a full stretch) | Beat the seated raise by a wide margin: outer head +12.4% vs +1.7%, inner head +9.2% vs +0.6%. | A | Kinoshita2023 (FULL) |
| 2 | **Leg-press calf raise, knees straight** | The exercise used in the range-of-motion trial: +6.7% with full reps and +15.2% with bottom-half reps in 8 weeks. Same straight knee as rank 1 and no load on the spine. | B | Kassiano2023b (ABSTRACT); Nunes2020b (ABSTRACT) |
| 3 | **Single-leg standing calf raise** (calf machine, or a dumbbell on a step) | Single-leg machine raises grew the gastrocnemius 8% in 10 weeks. The dumbbell version is untested and limited by grip and balance. | B | Goli2026 (ABSTRACT); Larsen2025b (ABSTRACT) |
| 4 | **Donkey calf raise** | No study found. Straight knee, so expect it to match rank 1. | C | none found |

**How to do every rep (this matters more than which machine):**

- **Use the bottom.** Bottom-half reps beat top-half reps, +15.2% vs +3.4% (Kassiano2023b). Adding bottom-half
  partials after failure gave +9.6% vs +6.7% in beginners (Larsen2025d, ABSTRACT). In trained lifters the edge was
  0.40 mm with an interval that crosses zero (Larsen2025b). So: full stretch every rep, and bottom partials when you
  can no longer reach the top. Do not expect a large bonus.
- **Partials save sets.** Half as many sets, each extended with bottom partials, grew the calf as much as the full
  number of normal sets: 8% vs 8% (Goli2026).
- **More sets help.** 12 sets a week beat 6: outer head +14.3% vs +8.1%, soleus +12.7% vs +6.7% (Kassiano2024,
  ABSTRACT; untrained women, 6 weeks).
- **Foot angle is a minor detail.** Toes out favoured the inner head (+8.4% vs +3.8%); toes in favoured the outer
  head (+9.1% vs +5.5%). One trial, never repeated (Nunes2020b).

**Skip or demote**

- **Seated calf raise for the gastrocnemius** — D. +0.6% and +1.7% in 12 weeks (Kinoshita2023).
- **Bouncy top-half reps** — D. +3.4% (Kassiano2023b).

### 5b. Soleus (the flat muscle under the gastrocnemius)

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Standing calf raise, knee straight** | Tied the seated raise for soleus growth (+2.1% vs +2.9%, p = 0.410) while winning for the gastrocnemius. One exercise covers both. | A | Kinoshita2023 (FULL) |
| 2 | **Seated calf raise** | Tied rank 1 for the soleus and lost for everything else. A bent knee gives more soleus swelling right after a session, but that did not turn into more growth. | A | Kinoshita2023 (FULL); Kassiano2023c (ABSTRACT) |

**Also fine**

- **A 20-second loaded stretch at the bottom between sets** — soleus +0.7 mm more (90% CI 0 to 1.6), nothing for the
  gastrocnemius (VanEvery2022, ABSTRACT; 21 untrained men). Uncertain. Free to try.

### 5c. Tibialis anterior (front of the shin)

## 6. Abs and trunk

**No training study compares one ab exercise with another for muscle size, and none compares direct ab work with
compound lifts only.** Two days of searching agree on that. Every rank below except one rests on EMG.

### 6a. Rectus abdominis (the six-pack)

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cable crunch (kneeling) or machine crunch** (round the spine, ribs to pelvis; do not just hinge at the hips) | The curl-up is the only ab exercise with any size measurement: a 4-week bodyweight curl-up programme raised rectus abdominis area on MRI. The cable and machine versions are the same movement with a weight stack, so you can train it in the 8–15 rep range and add load. Weak B. | B | RutkowskaKucharska2018 (ABSTRACT) |
| 2 | **Ab wheel rollout** (from the knees, hips tucked, go as far as you can hold a flat back) | Rollouts topped two lab comparisons. On a wheel, the rollout was in the highest group for upper and lower abs. On a Swiss ball it reached 63% (upper) and 53% (lower) of maximum, against 7–53% and 7–44% for most other exercises, with little hip-flexor activity. Works the abs while they lengthen. | C | Escamilla2006 (ABSTRACT); Escamilla2010 (ABSTRACT) |
| 3 | **Hanging knee raise or leg raise** (curl the pelvis up at the top; a swinging straight-leg lift is mostly hip flexors) | Among the highest for upper and lower abs and obliques. Also works the hip flexors hard. | C | Escamilla2006 (ABSTRACT) |
| 4 | **Reverse crunch on a 30° incline** | In the top group for upper and lower abs. | C | Escamilla2006 (ABSTRACT) |
| 5 | **Decline weighted sit-up** | Loadable, but the bent-knee sit-up was not in the top group for ab EMG and had high hip-flexor (rectus femoris) activity. | C | Escamilla2006 (ABSTRACT) |

**No data separates ranks 2–5 for growth.** Rank 1 is first because it is the easiest to load and progress like any
other muscle, and because its unloaded version is the only one with a size measurement. That is thin.

### 6b. Obliques

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ab wheel rollout and hanging knee raise** (the same ones as above) | Highest external oblique EMG in the lab comparisons: wheel exercises and the hanging knee-up in one, and 46% of maximum for the Swiss-ball rollout (84% for the ball pike) in the other. You get oblique work without a separate exercise. | C | Escamilla2006 (ABSTRACT); Escamilla2010 (ABSTRACT) |
| 2 | **Side bend, cable woodchop, Pallof press, Russian twist** | No training study and no EMG record retrieved for any of them today. Anatomy only: they bend or twist the trunk against load. | C | none found |

**Also fine**

- **Heavy squats, deadlifts and presses** — in an EMG review, free-weight exercises gave the highest rectus
  abdominis, external oblique and erector activity (OlivaLozano2020, ABSTRACT). In trained men the rectus abdominis
  grew over 8 weeks of squat, bench, row and press with no ab-exercise arm for comparison (HernandezBelmonte2023).
  This does not show that direct ab work adds nothing.

**Skip or demote**

- **Plank** — no growth data, and bodyweight holds are hard to progress. Not shown inferior; just the least like how
  you train any other muscle for size.
- **Side bridge on a wheel** — loads one side of the rectus abdominis more than a plain side bridge (Kawama2022,
  ABSTRACT). A niche tool for evening out a left-right difference.

**Evidence notes**

- RutkowskaKucharska2018 (ABSTRACT): the abstract gives no sample size and no numbers, has no control group, and its
  results and conclusion disagree on whether "most" or "all" muscles grew. It is the only study of its kind found.
- Escamilla2006 (21 adults; wheel device, hanging straps, crunch, sit-up) and Escamilla2010 (18 adults; Swiss-ball
  rollout and pike vs crunch and sit-up): bodyweight EMG. The cable crunch, machine crunch and weighted sit-up were
  not tested in either.
- SanchisMoysi2010 (ABSTRACT): professional tennis players had 58% more rectus abdominis volume than inactive men,
  and 35% more on the non-dominant side. Cross-sectional, 8 players. It shows the muscle can grow a lot with years of
  hard trunk work. It does not show which gym exercise does it.

## 7. Lower back / spinal erectors

**No study measured erector size after deadlifts, back extensions or reverse hypers in healthy lifters.** One 1992
trial shows the erectors grow with direct trunk training but does not name the exercises. Everything below is EMG.

| Rank | Exercise (exact name and setup) | Why it ranks here | Grade | Key study (key, access) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **45° back extension** (the same one as hamstrings rank 2; let the spine round a little and extend if you want more erector work) | Erector EMG equal to the Romanian deadlift and the seated machine (p = 1.00), with the most hamstring and glute activity of the three. One exercise, three muscle groups. | C | Andersen2021 (ABSTRACT) |
| 2 | **Romanian deadlift** | Same erector EMG as rank 1. Erectors work less than the hamstrings here. Heavy loading. | C | Andersen2021 (ABSTRACT); MartinFuentes2020 (ABSTRACT) |
| 3 | **Conventional deadlift** | In the EMG review the erectors and quads were the most active muscles, above glutes and hamstrings. Erector EMG no different from the hex-bar deadlift or hip thrust. High fatigue cost for a C-grade pick. | C | MartinFuentes2020 (ABSTRACT); Andersen2018 (ABSTRACT) |
| 4 | **Reverse hyper** | Two studies disagree: one found 28–71% more EMG than the back extension in all three muscles (10 people), the other found equal erector activity and less glute and hamstring work (20 people). | C | Cuthbert2021 (ABSTRACT); Lawrence2019 (ABSTRACT) |

**No data separates these.** The order is practical: rank 1 doubles as a hamstring pick and costs the least fatigue.

**Also fine**

- **Seated back-extension machine** — same erector EMG as ranks 1 and 2, less glute and hamstring (Andersen2021).
- **Front squat** — more iliocostalis EMG than back squat variations in 10 bodybuilders (Coratella2021). EMG only.

**Skip or demote**

- **Adding a dedicated lower-back exercise on top of squats and Romanian deadlifts** — probably redundant. No trial
  says so; it is a fatigue judgment.

**Evidence notes**

- Parkkola1992 (ABSTRACT): 12 sedentary students; 18 weeks of trunk strength training; MRI at L4–L5. Back-muscle
  area rose (P < 0.01) and psoas area rose (P < 0.001). No percentages, no control, exercises unnamed.

## The short list

- **Quads (vasti):** deep high-bar back squat, or leg press / Smith squat if you prefer. No data separates them.
- **Quads (rectus femoris):** leg extension, leaning back.
- **Hamstrings:** seated leg curl first; 45° back extension or Romanian deadlift second. Nordic curl if the short
  head or semitendinosus lags.
- **Glute max:** your deep squat or leg press already counts; barbell hip thrust if you want more.
- **Upper glute / glute medius:** standing cable hip abduction, or the seated abduction machine.
- **Adductors:** deep squat or leg press covers the adductor magnus; adduction machine or Copenhagen for the rest.
- **Calves (gastrocnemius and soleus):** standing calf raise, knee straight, full stretch; leg-press calf raise as the
  second option. No seated calf raise needed.
- **Tibialis:** tibialis raise, only if you care.
- **Abs:** cable crunch, plus ab wheel rollout or hanging knee raise. All on thin evidence.
- **Lower back:** 45° back extension or Romanian deadlift, which you are already doing for hamstrings.
- **Hip flexors:** hanging leg raise. Optional.

## Sample week of picks

Two leg sessions. Eleven exercises cover every muscle above. For sets and effort use the top-5 report (about 10–20
hard sets per muscle per week, most sets 1–3 reps short of failure). Keep the same exercises for a whole block.

**Session 1**

| # | Exercise | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Barbell back squat, below parallel | vasti, glute max, adductor magnus |
| 2 | Seated leg curl | biceps femoris long and short head, semitendinosus, semimembranosus |
| 3 | Leg extension, leaning back | rectus femoris, plus more vasti work |
| 4 | Standing calf raise, straight knee | gastrocnemius and soleus |
| 5 | Cable crunch | rectus abdominis |

**Session 2**

| # | Exercise | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Leg press (or hack squat) | vasti, glute max, adductor magnus |
| 2 | 45° back extension, loaded (or Romanian deadlift) | biceps femoris long head, semimembranosus, glutes, erectors |
| 3 | Leg extension, leaning back | rectus femoris |
| 4 | Standing cable hip abduction (or abduction machine) | upper glute max, gluteus medius and minimus |
| 5 | Leg-press calf raise, straight knee | gastrocnemius and soleus |
| 6 | Ab wheel rollout or hanging knee raise | rectus abdominis, obliques, hip flexors |

**What doubles up, so you do not need a separate exercise**

- Deep squat and leg press each count for quads, glute max and adductor magnus.
- The back extension or Romanian deadlift counts for hamstrings, glutes and lower back.
- The standing calf raise counts for gastrocnemius and soleus.
- Rollouts and hanging raises count for abs, obliques and hip flexors.

**Add only if a body part lags**

- Glutes: barbell hip thrust or cable kickback.
- Semitendinosus or short head of the biceps femoris: Nordic curl.
- Front inner thigh: adduction machine or Copenhagen adduction.
- Shins: tibialis raise.

**Leave out:** seated calf raise, lying leg curl when the seated one is free, half squats, top-half leg extensions,
and a third squat-type lift.

## New avenues

1. **Named-exercise trials in trained lifters.** Seated vs lying curl, standing vs seated calf raise, leaning-back vs
   upright leg extension and squat vs leg press vs hack squat have still only been run in beginners, or not at all.
   Check trial registries and 2027 preprints.
2. **Abs.** Does a loaded crunch grow the rectus abdominis more than a rollout, a leg raise, or no direct work? No
   trial exists. Search theses, non-indexed journals and the electrical-stimulation literature for usable imaging.
3. **Machines nobody has tested for growth:** hip adduction, hip abduction (and leaning forward on it), hack squat,
   pendulum squat. One 2026 preprint on abduction machine angles appeared in a listing and was not read.
4. **45° back extension vs Romanian deadlift vs seated curl for the biceps femoris long head,** and whether the
   "hamstring-biased" and "glute-biased" ways of doing the back extension change which muscle grows.

## Doubts / audit flags

- **57 of the 59 new rows are ABSTRACT only.** Only Kinoshita2026 and Hermann2026 were read in full (methods, results
  tables, limitations, funding). For AlonsoFernandez2022, Goli2026 and MartinSanAgustin2025 I consulted parts of the
  full text and still mark them ABSTRACT. Publisher sites were blocked.
- **"Hip extension exercise" = 45° back extension is SECONDARY** for Bourne2017 and Carmichael2022. It comes from the
  methods text of MartinSanAgustin2025. Hamstrings rank 2 depends on it. The auditor should try both full texts.
- **Lazarczuk2024 is probably Bourne2017's trial re-analysed** (same design, group sizes and duration). Not
  confirmed. I counted it as the same evidence.
- **B grades given "by principle", with no study of the exact exercise:** hack squat, seated hip abduction machine,
  cable/machine crunch, single-leg dumbbell calf raise, tibialis raise, cable hip flexion. A stricter reader would
  call the hack squat, abduction machine and cable crunch C.
- **Weak studies carrying a pick:** AlonsoFernandez2019 (reverse Nordic) and AlonsoFernandez2022 (Copenhagen) come
  from one lab, have weak or no controls, and report effect sizes of 1.8–3.1 with a full return to baseline in 4
  weeks. RutkowskaKucharska2018 (curl-up) gives no sample size and no numbers. Yamanaka2021 has 8 runners.
- **Conflicts inside the evidence.** Frouin2024 found no long-head growth from seated curl plus stiff-leg deadlift,
  against Maeo2021. Chiu2025 found no glute growth from squats, against Kubo2019 and Plotkin2023. Cuthbert2021 and
  Lawrence2019 disagree on the reverse hyper. The Earp2015 abstract contradicts itself on which thigh region grew.
- **Kinoshita2026's leg press went only to 90° of knee bend** and still grew the glute max 15.4%. That is more than
  squat trials found (6.7% in Kubo2019). Untrained people, one study. The size is uncertain.
- **Plotkin2026 is ahead of print**, with 16 men and one muscle imaged. Its DOI resolves on Crossref without volume or
  pages. Funding and conflicts are not in the abstract.
- **Industry links.** Hermann2026 was funded by a fitness company owned by a co-author. Gschneidner2025's authors work
  for commercial gym chains. Plotkin2023 has the hip-thrust conflicts described yesterday.
- **Exercises with no record of any kind retrieved today:** sissy squat, pendulum squat, goblet squat, heel-elevated
  squat, good morning, glute-ham raise, cable pull-through, standing single-leg curl, donkey calf raise, lateral band
  walk, side bend, cable woodchop, Pallof press, Russian twist. They are graded C on anatomy, or left unranked. A
  belt-squat EMG paper and a hip-thrust EMG review appeared in listings and were not read.
- **Key clashes with the older top-5 files.** In `top5/TOP5-STUDIES.csv`, "Larsen2025a" and "Kassiano2023a/b" are
  different papers from the ones with those keys in `STUDIES.csv`. This note uses the `STUDIES.csv` keys. The
  untrained calf-partials paper is Larsen2025d here.
- **Kassiano2023b numbers.** I re-read the PubMed abstract today: inner head +15.2%, +6.7%, +3.4%; outer head
  +14.9%, +7.3%, +6.2%. Yesterday's note is right; the older top-5 note mixed the two heads.
- **Two percentages are my own arithmetic** from Kinoshita2026's table of mean volumes: gluteus medius +4.9% and
  hamstrings +3.2% with the leg press. The paper's text gives a range of +1.5% to +5.3% for the hamstrings.
- **"No study found" claims** rest on about 60 PubMed and Europe PMC queries today plus yesterday's 30. WebSearch,
  Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex were not used. Journals outside those indexes could hold something.
- **Retractions and corrections.** All 57 new DOIs resolved on Crossref today with no update notice. Two rows
  (Neto2020, Escamilla2006) have no DOI and were checked on PubMed only. Escamilla2010 has a published comment and
  reply that I did not read.
