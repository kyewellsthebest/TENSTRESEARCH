# Protein powders: whey vs casein vs plant

**Verdict:** total daily protein matters; the powder doesn't. At matched intake, whey ≈ soy for muscle and strength.
Whey is the cheapest and cleanest default. Confidence **Moderate**. Last updated 2026-11-15, audited.

## What to do
- **Hit the total first:** about 1.6 g/kg (about 140 g/day) is the average point where extra protein stopped helping.
  Up to 2.2 g/kg (about 190 g) is a sensible upper target, especially when cutting. Use powder only if it makes that
  easier or cheaper.
- **Default:** whey concentrate or isolate, 30–40 g protein per serve. No need for hydrolysates, added BCAAs,
  "anabolic" blends or casein-only products.
- **If you go plant:** use soy isolate or a pea + rice blend, about 10–20% more per serve. Avoid chocolate flavours
  (more lead and cadmium), and prefer brands that publish heavy-metal batch results.
- **Buying in Australia:** use established brands, and avoid grey imports and "proprietary blend" or added-amino-acid
  labels (signs of amino spiking). In a tested federation, use only HASTA or Informed Sport batches.

## Key numbers
| Question | Finding (95% CI) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Protein supplementation, 49 RCTs | Fat-free mass +0.30 kg (0.09 to 0.52); 1RM +2.49 kg (0.64 to 4.33) | Morton 2018 |
| Trained lifters | Fat-free mass +1.05 kg (0.61 to 1.50); 1RM +4.27 kg (0.61 to 7.94) | Morton 2018 |
| Plateau | 1.62 g/kg/day (1.03 to 2.20), p = 0.079 | Morton 2018 |
| Plant vs animal, muscle mass | SMD −0.20 (−0.37 to −0.03) | Reid-McCann 2025 |
| Soy vs milk | SMD −0.02 (−0.20 to 0.16); no difference | Reid-McCann 2025 |
| Non-soy plant (5 trials) | SMD −0.58 (−1.06 to −0.09) | Reid-McCann 2025 |
| Vegan + soy vs omnivore + whey | Both about +1.2 kg leg lean mass | Hevia-Larraín 2021 |
| Australian supplements bought online | 35% of 200 products contained banned substances; 1 of 14 protein powders did | Barker 2025 |

## Caveats
- Head-to-head trials in trained lifters are tiny. The rice-vs-whey trials were funded by a rice-protein maker
  (Axiom).
- Dairy (Phillips, Volek) and soy (Messina) funding run through the field; Schoenfeld disclosed Dymatize advising in a
  2020 correction to Morton 2018.
- The heavy-metal figures (Clean Label Project; Consumer Reports 2025) are not peer-reviewed. No Australian survey was
  found.
- The "2–3 g leucine per meal" threshold was not verified this run.
