# Creatine monohydrate

**Verdict:** the best-evidenced legal supplement for lifters, though the gain is modest. Confidence **Strong** (muscle, strength,
kidney safety); **Limited** (cognition, non-responders). Last updated 2026-11-15, audited.

> **Deep dive in progress (phase 2 of 6: screening, 3,200 of 4,587 records screened, 2026-11-15).** The verdict below comes from the round-1 survey. A full systematic-review-style deep dive is under way (5,553 records found; 112 likely-eligible studies and reviews so far). The verdict will be updated as it progresses. Progress: [`knowledge-base/deep/creatine/progress.md`](../../knowledge-base/deep/creatine/progress.md)

## What to do
- Take **5 g of creatine monohydrate every day**, rest days included, at whatever time you'll remember. Timing makes
  no difference.
- **Loading is optional.** 20 g/day (4 × 5 g) for 5–7 days saturates muscle in about a week; without loading,
  5 g/day gets you there in about 3–4 weeks.
- **Expect about +1 kg on the scale** early on. It's mostly water inside the muscle, not fat. Recalibrate your weight
  trend; don't cut calories for it.
- **Tell your GP before any blood test.** Creatinine reads high and eGFR reads low. A cystatin C test gives a true
  kidney reading.
- Plain monohydrate only (not HCl, Kre-Alkalyn or blends). If you ever compete in a tested sport, buy Informed Sport-
  or HASTA-tested batches.

|---|---|---|
| Fat-free mass, 61 trials | +1.39 kg (1.07 to 1.70) | Ashtary-Larky 2025 |
| Trained vs untrained | +1.82 kg (1.10 to 2.55) vs +1.23 kg (0.91 to 1.56); not significantly different | Ashtary-Larky 2025 |
| Lean mass, no-industry-ties team | +1.14 kg (0.69 to 1.59) | Desai 2024 |
| Direct muscle size (MRI/CT/ultrasound) | SMD 0.11 (−0.02 to 0.25); about +0.10–0.16 cm thickness | Burke 2023 |
| Strength | g ≈ 0.54, about 0.35 after publication-bias correction | Wang 2026 |
| Strength in kg | Bench +1.43 kg (0.53 to 2.34); squat +5.64 kg (3.87 to 7.40) | Kazeminasab 2025 |
| Kidneys | Creatinine about +0.13 mg/dL and estimated GFR −10.75 mL/min, but measured GFR doesn't change: a test artefact | Tsiaras 2026; de Souza Almeida 2026; Lugaresi 2013 |

## Caveats
- Much of the field is industry-linked (AlzChem/Creapure advisers: Candow, Forbes, Antonio, Tinsley). The conclusions
  still hold in meta-analyses by teams with no declared conflicts.
- Direct muscle-size evidence is small and includes only 2 trials in trained lifters. Part of the "lean mass" gain is
  water.
- Gu 2026 (+3.39 kg FFM) is an implausible outlier and is rated very limited.
- The "20–30% are non-responders" figure rests on one 11-person biopsy study (Syrotuik 2004).
- The meta-analyses share many of the same trials, so their agreement isn't independent replication.
