# Lower-body exercise selection: which exercise grows more muscle and strength?

**Verdict:** there is no single best leg exercise. Each compound lift grows some muscles and leaves others behind, so
the useful answer is which gaps to fill: squats grow the rectus femoris much less than leg extensions do, squats do
not grow the hamstrings, seated leg curls beat lying ones, and standing calf raises beat seated ones. Free weights
and machines grow the same muscle. For most direct "A vs B" questions (back squat vs hack squat vs leg press, front
vs back squat, deadlift or lunges for glutes) no training study exists. Confidence **Moderate** for quads,
hamstrings and calves; **Limited** for glutes; **Very limited** for abs; **Strong** for strength being lift-specific.
Last updated 2026-11-16, audited. Nearly every trial used beginners.

## What to do
- **Quads, two exercises:** a deep squat-pattern lift you can load hard (back squat, hack squat or leg press; none is
  shown to beat the others), plus leg extensions, leaning back if the seat allows. If you only squat, expect the
  rectus femoris (front-middle of the thigh) to lag.
- **Hamstrings, two exercises:** a seated leg curl (a lying curl still works, just less well), plus a hip hinge such
  as a Romanian deadlift. Don't count squats, leg press or hip thrusts as hamstring work.
- **Glutes:** your deep squat or leg press already counts. Add hip thrusts if you want more glute work with less quad
  and lower-back fatigue. A hip-abduction exercise targets the upper glute.
- **Calves:** standing or leg-press calf raises with the knee straight, sinking into a full stretch at the bottom.
  Skip the seated calf raise or keep it as a minor extra.
- **Free weights or machines:** choose by comfort and equipment. Keep the barbell squat only if you care about your
  squat number.
- **Variety:** keep the same exercises for a whole block. Pick two per muscle group that hit different heads; don't
  rotate between look-alike lifts.

## What has never been tested for muscle growth
No training study with a muscle-size outcome was found for any of these (PubMed and Europe PMC, 2026-11-16):
- front vs back squat; hack squat vs anything; squat vs leg press with quad imaging;
- deadlifts, lunges, split squats or step-ups isolated for glute size;
- leg curl vs Romanian or stiff-leg deadlift head-to-head;
- direct ab training vs compound lifts only, or one ab exercise against another;
- any adductor-specific exercise.

## Caveats
- Almost every head-to-head trial is in untrained people over 6–12 weeks with 8–20 per group. For a trained lifter the
  direction is the best available guess; the size is uncertain.
- The calf and seated-curl results each rest on one small trial (14 and 20 people).
- The hip thrust vs squat trial had hip-thrust-industry involvement: a 2026 correction added that the hip thrust
  equipment was donated and that the trial was registered after the fact. No data changed.
- Two trials in trained women that favour squats over hip thrusts (Barbalho 2020, 2021) are given no weight. Other
  papers from that group have been retracted, although these two carry no retraction notice.
- A first-session EMG reading favoured the hip thrust but did not predict growth. EMG is not evidence of growth.
- 31 of the 38 lower-body papers were read as abstracts only, because publisher sites were blocked.
