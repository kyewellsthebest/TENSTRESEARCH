# Upper-body exercise selection: which exercise grows more muscle and strength?

**Verdict:** for most upper-body muscles, research cannot rank one exercise above another. Only a handful of exercise
pairs have ever been compared in a training study that measured muscle size, and most of those used beginners for
8–12 weeks. The clear results are few: overhead triceps extensions beat arm-at-side extensions, pressing alone leaves
the side delt and the triceps long head behind, and strength goes to the lift you train. **No study compares any two
back exercises for muscle growth**, so lat pulldown vs pull-up vs rows is unanswered. Confidence **Limited** overall
(**Strong** for strength being lift-specific; **Moderate** for barbell vs machine pressing). Last updated 2026-11-16,
audited.

## What to do
- **Chest:** pick one heavy press you can progress for years: barbell, Smith or a good machine grow the pecs about
  equally. Use the barbell bench if you care about your bench number. Add a low incline press (about 30–45°) as a
  sensible bet for the upper pec. A fly is optional; nothing shows it beats or adds to pressing.
- **Back:** one vertical pull (pulldown or pull-up) and one row. Choose the version and grip you can load hard without
  elbow or shoulder pain. Nothing shows that grip width, overhand vs underhand, or pulldown vs pull-up matters for
  size. Don't count pullovers as lat work.
- **Shoulders:** do lateral raises for the side delt, dumbbell or cable (they are equal). Don't count bench pressing
  toward side delts. Your pressing already covers the front delt. A reverse fly for rear delts is cheap insurance, but
  it is untested.
- **Arms:** do direct curls and don't rely on rows for biceps. Any curl works. For triceps, make an **overhead cable
  extension** your first choice, and don't rely on pressing or pushdowns alone.
- **Strength:** keep the lifts you want to be strong in and train them every week. Swap accessories freely, but not
  every session.

## What has never been tested for muscle growth
No training study with a muscle-size outcome was found for any of these (PubMed and Europe PMC, 2026-11-16):
- any back comparison: lat pulldown vs pull-up, pulldown vs row, grip width or type, pullover (a 2026 review of 23 lat
  EMG studies confirms none ran a training study);
- dumbbell vs barbell bench, decline bench, bench grip width, bench vs flyes, cable crossover or pec deck;
- overhead press vs lateral raise for the side delt; rear-delt exercises vs rows;
- forearm exercises;
- incline vs flat bench in trained lifters.

For these, EMG is all there is, and an EMG difference is not proof of more growth.

## Caveats
- Most trials are in beginners, 8–12 weeks long, with 10–40 people. Only a few used trained lifters. Treat the
  direction of each result as the best available guess and the size as uncertain.
- "No difference" from a small trial is not proof of equality. The lateral-raise result is the exception: it was
  tested for equivalence.
- The triceps result rests on one study that used a cable only, and it has not been repeated in trained lifters.
- A group of compound-vs-isolation trials (Barbalho and colleagues) is given no weight here: two are retracted and a
  third has a 2026 expression of concern.
- 46 of the 57 upper-body papers were read as abstracts only, because publisher sites were blocked.
- Effort, weekly sets and progression matter far more than which press or curl you pick. See
  [`top-5-factors.md`](../training/top-5-factors.md).
