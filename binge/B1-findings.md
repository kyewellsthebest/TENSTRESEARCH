# B1 — What happens to ~1,000 extra kcal in one day

Agent B1, 2026-11-08. Brief: `BRIEF.md`. Studies: `B1-studies.csv` (32 rows). Searches (34): `B1-searchlog.md`.
Builds on `../fatloss/W1-findings.md` and `../fatloss/WA-audit.md`. Their glycogen-water and refeed results are reused, not re-searched.

**Access.** Almost everything here is ABSTRACT: the abstract was read through search results. `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` could not be reached (WebFetch: ENOTFOUND, host not in the session's allowed network domains), so no PMC full text was opened. Where a number comes only from a press release, blog or citing paper, it is marked SECONDARY. Numbers I could not confirm are marked UNVERIFIED.

**Evidence codes** (PROTOCOL §5): **I** = indirect human evidence. These are controlled feeding studies, but not Kye's exact pattern (a lean lifter in a deficit, one surplus day a week). **M** = mechanistic or arithmetic reasoning. Nothing here is **D**. No study has fed trained people in a deficit +1,000 kcal on one day a week and measured fat mass.

## Plain-English summary

1. **Yes, the Sunday adds fat, but only about 0.1 kg of it.** Of 1,000 extra kcal, about 50–250 kcal is burned off on the day as extra heat from digesting and processing the food. The other **750–950 kcal stays in the body**. As pure fat that is about **80–100 g**. As body fat tissue, which is not 100 % fat, it is about **100–125 g**. A protein-heavy surplus is probably at the low end and a fat-heavy one at the high end. *(I, moderate)*
2. **It is not a separate lump of fat to be burned off later.** It comes straight out of the week's deficit. Over the following deficit days it is simply part of the energy balance.
3. **The arithmetic decides how much it matters.** On the 0.25 kg/week fat-loss target in the earlier notes, the 5 deficit days are worth about 1,900 kcal. One +1,000 kcal Sunday takes back **about 40–50 %** of that week's fat loss. That is not a disaster, but it is a large share. On a bigger deficit it is a smaller share.
4. **Monday "feeling fatter" is almost entirely not fat.** A high-carb, salty day refills glycogen, and each gram of glycogen holds about 3 g of water. It also leaves food and fluid in the gut, and salt can add extracellular water. Together these plausibly add **0.5–2.5 kg** for 2–5 days. That is 5–20 times the actual fat gain.
5. **Why the scale "reads the same".** The week's real loss (about 0.1–0.3 kg) is hidden under that temporary water and gut weight on Monday. Weight is expected to peak on Sunday or Monday and fall through the week. Dieters who are losing weight show exactly this pattern (Orsama 2014). The ~0.1 kg of real fat, spread across the whole body, cannot be seen or felt. Bloating, gut distension and puffiness can.
6. **"A cheat day boosts metabolism" is true only trivially.** Energy expenditure rises by a few percent on the day, and that rise is the 5–25 % of the surplus that never gets stored. Leptin rises by about 25–40 % for roughly a day. No study shows a lasting rise in metabolism, or extra fat loss, from it. **A big day never burns more than it adds.** *(I, moderate)*
7. **A binge "here and there" is physiologically fine.** The cost is arithmetic and capped at about 0.1 kg of fat per +1,000 kcal. Reverting to normal eating sorts out the water and gut weight within days.
8. **What matters is the behaviour, not the physiology.** A *planned* high day is a diet tactic. Eating that feels out of control is different, especially if it is followed by distress and happens about weekly. That can be a sign of binge eating disorder, which is common and treatable. In Australia the first steps are a GP or the Butterfly Foundation helpline, **1800 33 4673**. See section 9.

## 1. Where 1,000 extra kcal goes in the first 24 h

There are three possible fates for the extra energy: burned as extra heat on the day, stored as glycogen, or stored as fat. Separately, a little protein is laid down as lean tissue.

**1a. How much is burned off.** These are measurements of diet-induced thermogenesis, the extra heat from processing food:
- **Thearle 2013** is the best single-day study. n = 20, each person did seven 24-h sessions in a whole-room calorimeter. Overfeeding at 200 % of needs (20 % protein) raised 24-h energy expenditure by **10.7 ± 5.7 % (range 2.9–18.8 %)**. The response was reproducible within a person and smaller in fatter people (r = −0.53). Doubling a ~2,500 kcal intake therefore costs about 270 kcal, roughly **11 % of the surplus**. The authors call it "a small percentage of consumed calories". *(I, moderate; ABSTRACT)*
- **Dallosso & James 1984**: 1 week of fat overfeeding (+~50 %), n = 8 men. 24-h expenditure rose 5.6–6.4 %, which is **9–11 % of the surplus**. *(I, limited)*
- **Schutz 1989**: +987 ± 55 kcal/d of fat did not change 24-h expenditure measurably, nor fat oxidation. *(I, limited; n not seen)*
- **Horton 1995** (14 d, +50 %, 16 men): carbohydrate surplus **75–85 % stored**, fat surplus **90–95 % stored**. So 15–25 % of a carbohydrate surplus is dissipated, against 5–10 % of a fat surplus. The difference was largest early in overfeeding, which is the part relevant to a single day. *(I, moderate)*
- Protein has the highest thermic effect. In Bray 2012 the normal- and high-protein surpluses raised resting expenditure and lean mass (see 1d).

**Working range for Kye:** 50–250 kcal of the 1,000 is dissipated. **750–950 kcal is retained.**

**1b. Carbohydrate is stored as glycogen and burned first. It does not turn into fat.** Carbohydrate balance is tightly regulated:
- **Jebb 1996** (12-d continuous calorimetry, +33 %): carbohydrate oxidation matched intake by about day 5 (540 g eaten, 551 g oxidised). **Fat oxidation fell to 59 g/d**, and fat balance absorbed **74 % of the energy surplus**. The authors describe an "oxidative hierarchy" in which keeping carbohydrate in balance comes first.
- The practical point: **extra carbohydrate makes you store fat by switching off the burning of the fat you eat**, not by converting carbs into fat.

**1c. Converting carbohydrate into fat (de novo lipogenesis, DNL) is small after one day.** *(I, moderate)*
- **Acheson 1988** (n = 3): it took glycogen depletion followed by days of 3,600–4,900 kcal/d at about 86 % carbohydrate to saturate glycogen. Capacity was about 15 g/kg, and roughly 500 g could be stored before net lipogenesis began. Only then did DNL reach about 150 g of fat a day.
- **McDevitt 2001**: 96 h at +50 % sugar raised DNL 2–3-fold in lean and obese women, but it "does not contribute greatly to total fat balance".
- **Minehira 2004** (4 d at 175 % of needs, 71 % carbohydrate): overweight subjects showed lower whole-body DNL and more glycogen synthesis than lean subjects.
- A +1,000 kcal day after 5 deficit days is nowhere near glycogen saturation. Direct conversion of carbohydrate to fat is a few grams at most. *(M)*

**1d. Fat is stored almost one-for-one.**
- Adding fat to a meal or a day does not raise fat oxidation (Flatt 1985 SECONDARY; Schutz 1989).
- Dallosso & James found "substantial fat deposition".
- So the fat in a pizza-and-ice-cream day goes almost straight into adipose tissue. *(I, moderate)*

**1e. Protein: some goes to lean tissue, and less is stored as fat.**
- **Bray 2012** (8 wk, +954 kcal/d, inpatient RCT, n = 25):
  - Body fat gain was similar at 5 %, 15 % and 25 % protein.
  - The low-protein group stored more than 90 % of the excess as fat. The normal- and high-protein groups stored about half as fat and half as lean tissue (from secondary summaries; per-group fat kg not confirmed).
  - Weight gain was 3.16 kg on low protein, 6.05 on normal and 6.51 on high.
- **Antonio 2014** (4.4 g/kg/d, n = 30 trained) and **Antonio 2015** (3.4 g/kg/d, n = 48 trained, more total energy than controls): no fat gain, or even fat loss, despite eating more. These are self-reported, free-living and small. Non-significance here is not proof of equivalence, and the same lab wrote the main review (Leaf & Antonio 2017).
- **Conclusion:** a protein-heavy surplus probably stores less fat per kcal. How much less is *limited* evidence.

## 2. Realistic fat gain from Kye's +1,000 kcal Sunday

These are energy-balance calculations *(M)* built on the section 1 data. Pure triglyceride is about 9.4 kcal/g. Adipose tissue is about 7,700 kcal/kg, because it is only about 85 % lipid.

| surplus mix | fraction retained | retained kcal | as pure fat | as adipose tissue | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fat-heavy (pizza, ice cream, ~50 % fat) | 90–95 % (Horton) | 900–950 | 95–100 g | 115–125 g | most goes straight to fat by Monday |
| mixed "Western" | ~85–90 % | 850–900 | 90–95 g | 110–115 g | |
| carb-heavy, glycogen not full | 75–85 % (Horton) | 750–850 | 80–90 g | 100–110 g | **part sits as glycogen on Monday**, so the immediate fat gain is lower (perhaps 30–70 g, UNVERIFIED estimate); the glycogen is burned instead of fat over the next days, and the **weekly** cost converges on this row |
| protein-forward | maybe 60–80 % (Bray, Antonio; limited) | 600–800 | 65–85 g | 80–105 g | some to lean tissue; weakest evidence |

**Bottom line: about 50–125 g of adipose tissue per +1,000 kcal day, central about 100 g.** Glycogen is a timing buffer, not a loophole. Energy stored as glycogen on Sunday is energy not taken from fat on Monday to Wednesday.

**Weekly arithmetic.** Assume 5 deficit days × D kcal and maintenance on Friday and Saturday:

| daily deficit D | week without Sunday surplus | week with +1,000 Sunday (750–950 retained) | share of the week's fat loss given back |
|---|---|---|---|
| 250 | 1,250 kcal ≈ 160 g adipose | 300–500 kcal ≈ 40–65 g | 60–75 % |
| 385 (0.25 kg/wk target) | 1,925 kcal ≈ 250 g | 975–1,175 kcal ≈ 125–155 g | 40–50 % |
| 500 | 2,500 kcal ≈ 325 g | 1,550–1,750 kcal ≈ 200–230 g | 30–40 % |

"Is it recovered within days?" At D = 385 it takes about 2–2.5 deficit days to cancel. At D = 250 it takes 3–4 days. The week still ends in a deficit in every row. **It would stop doing so only if the Sunday surplus grew toward 1,300–2,600 kcal**, depending on the deficit.

**Weeks-long context** *(I, moderate; not one-day data)*:
- **Levine 1999**: +1,000 kcal/d for 8 weeks (56,000 kcal), n = 16. Fat gain ranged **0.36–4.23 kg** (SECONDARY), and two-thirds of the rise in expenditure was NEAT (non-exercise movement such as fidgeting and posture).
- **Bouchard 1990**: +1,000 kcal on 6 days a week for 84 days (84,000 kcal), 12 identical-twin pairs. Mean weight gain 8.1 kg, with ~3× more variance between pairs than within them.
- These show huge individual spread, but they describe a sustained surplus. Whether NEAT rises after a single day was not measured: chambers confine people. Do not assume a one-day NEAT rebate.
- The twins lost about 7 of the 8 kg by 4 months (Bouchard 1996, SECONDARY).

## 3. Glycogen, water, salt and gut contents: the Monday scale

**Glycogen** *(I, moderate)*:
- Five days in a deficit plus training leave muscle and liver glycogen below full.
- **Bussau 2002**: one day of high carbohydrate (10 g/kg) with rest took muscle glycogen from 95 to **180 mmol/kg wet mass, the maximum, within 24 h**, with no further rise on days 2–3 (n = 8 trained men).
- So one big carbohydrate day is enough to refill.
- Water: Fernández-Elías 2015 found at least 3 g of water per g of glycogen (W1). Olsson & Saltin 1970 implied 1–4 g/g and argued against a fixed ratio (SECONDARY).
- **Worked:** refilling 100–300 g of glycogen × (1 g + 3 g water) = **+0.4 to +1.2 kg**. *(M)*

**Sodium** *(I, limited; mixed)*:
- **Heer 2000** (n = 32 men, ward): raising salt to 550 mEq/d raised plasma volume up to +315 mL, but **not total body water or body mass**.
- **Heer 2009 ("Salty Life 6", n = 9)**: going from low to normal salt retained 193 mmol of sodium with **+2.02 L of extracellular volume**. Going from normal to high retained sodium without extra volume.
- Mars105 found sodium stores swinging without weight change (SECONDARY).
- None of these tested a single salty day. Arithmetic: 3 g of retained sodium ≈ 130 mmol ≈ 0.9 L of isotonic fluid. A realistic range for one salty restaurant/takeaway day is **0 to +1 kg**, and it is **very uncertain**. *(M)*

**Gut contents** *(M, limited)*:
- 1,000 extra kcal of mixed food and drink plausibly weighs 0.5–1.5 kg. That is an estimate: no study was found that measured it.
- Whole-gut transit is about 1–3 days, and stool output is about 100–300 g/day in the transit studies found (SECONDARY).
- Fibre-rich or high-volume food slows the clear-out of that weight.

**Sum:** Monday can plausibly sit **+0.5 to +2.5 kg above trend**, for **2–5 days**. That is about **5–20× the true fat gain**.
- **Orsama 2014** (n = 80, 4,657 daily weighings): weight rises from Saturday, peaks Sunday/Monday and falls from Tuesday. The weekly peak fell on Sunday or Monday in 59 % of weight losers. The compensation pattern was strongest in people losing or maintaining weight.
- **Helander 2016** (smart scales, n = 2,924) is a caution against repeated surpluses: holiday gains of 0.4–0.6 % were only about half reversed soon after.
- Wansink co-authored both of these. He has had other papers retracted; these two are not known to be among them.

**Why the scale "reads the same" on Monday.** It is the sum of a downward fat trend (−0.1 to −0.3 kg for the week) and a temporary upward swing. **Read Friday mornings, or a 7-day average of daily weights, not Mondays.**

## 4. Why he feels fatter

- **What can be seen or felt:** gut distension and gas, especially from fermentable carbs and large volumes; subcutaneous and facial puffiness from salt; a full stomach and lower abdomen. *(M)*
- **What cannot:** about 100 g of adipose tissue is roughly 110 mL, spread across the whole body's fat stores. For a lean man with about 10–15 kg of fat, that is under 1 % of it. *(M)*
- Refilled glycogen tends to make muscle look *fuller*, not fatter (W1: "flat" is low glycogen).
- The feeling is real, but it reports water and gut contents, not fat.

## 5. Leptin and appetite after one big day

- **Dirlewanger 2000** (10 lean women, 3 days each arm): carbohydrate overfeeding raised **leptin +28 %** and 24-h expenditure **+7 %**. Fat overfeeding did neither. Basal metabolic rate was unchanged, and **the leptin change was unrelated to the expenditure change**. *(I, limited)*
- **Kolaczynski 1996**: 12 h of heavy overfeeding raised leptin about 40 %, and it was still raised the next morning. *(I, limited)*
- Leptin falls again once the deficit resumes the next day (secondary commentary; no primary single-day study found).
- **Halliday 2020** (3 days at 140 %, n = 52): afterwards, ad-lib intake fell only in obesity-resistant adults (P = 0.03), not obesity-prone ones (P = 0.33). PYY rose only in the resistant group. Disinhibition predicted 5-year weight gain (r = 0.35).
- **Compensation after overfeeding is modest and varies by person.** Do not expect appetite to fall automatically on Monday. *(I, limited)*

## 6. Does an overfeed day "boost metabolism"? Fact-check

| claim | verdict | basis |
|---|---|---|
| A cheat or refeed day raises metabolic rate | **True but trivial**: +5–11 % of 24-h expenditure on the day, which is the 5–25 % of the surplus not stored | Thearle 2013; Dallosso 1984; Dirlewanger 2000; Horton 1995 |
| …so it pays for itself or speeds fat loss | **False.** No study shows expenditure rising by more than the surplus | all of the above; strong by energy balance |
| It "resets leptin" and restores metabolism in a diet | **Not supported.** Leptin rises 25–40 % transiently; it does not drive the expenditure change (Dirlewanger) | Kolaczynski 1996; Dirlewanger 2000 |
| Refeeds give more fat loss than continuous dieting | **Not shown in trained people.** Campbell 2020 (2-day weekly refeed, n = 27): no added fat loss; only dry FFM differed on reanalysis (via WA audit) | Campbell 2020; WA-audit |
| It restores the drop in metabolic rate | Only over weeks of refeeding: Müller 2015's mass-independent drop in resting expenditure reversed over **2 weeks** of refeeding, not 1 day | Müller 2015 (via W1) |

Possible real benefits are adherence and training quality, from full glycogen. These are plausible but **not quantified** here.

## 8. Is a binge "here and there" alright?

**Physiologically, yes.** *(I/M, moderate)*
- Each +1,000 kcal costs about 0.1 kg of fat, and nothing else lasts.
- Water and gut weight resolve within days.
- No metabolic harm or benefit beyond the day has been shown.

**Arithmetically, it depends on the deficit.**
- On a small deficit, a weekly 1,000 kcal Sunday takes back roughly half the week's fat loss.
- If the Sunday grows, or Friday and Saturday creep above maintenance, the week goes to zero quickly. Helander shows repeated surpluses are not fully undone.

**Practical levers** (inference, not trials):
- Plan the day.
- Keep protein high and fat moderate. Fat is stored most efficiently, though the difference is small against the weekly total.
- Judge progress by the Friday weight or a 7-day average.

## 9. Planned high day vs binge eating as a behaviour

These are different things.
- **A planned high-calorie day** is a deliberate dietary choice. It is eaten without a sense of being unable to stop, and without distress afterwards.
- **Binge eating** in the clinical sense has these features (DSM-5, SECONDARY reproduction):
  - **loss of control** while eating an amount definitely larger than most people would in a similar period;
  - three or more of: eating fast, eating until uncomfortably full, eating when not hungry, eating alone from embarrassment, feeling disgusted or guilty afterwards;
  - **marked distress**;
  - on average **at least once a week for 3 months** for binge eating disorder; less often still counts as a recognised sub-threshold pattern.
- Kye's pattern is weekly, so the distinction is worth an honest look. Regular dieting is also a known context for binge episodes (general knowledge; not searched here).
- This is not a diagnosis. Overeating on a Sunday is common and usually benign.

**When to seek help, in Australia.** If eating feels out of control, if it drives guilt, compensating (skipping meals, extra cardio, purging), or preoccupation, or if it is getting more frequent:
- Talk to a **GP**. GPs can refer to eating-disorder-informed psychological care. A Medicare-funded eating-disorder treatment plan exists, but its details were not checked in this search.
- Or call the **Butterfly Foundation National Helpline: 1800 33 4673**. Phone, webchat or email, 8 am–midnight AEST/AEDT, 7 days (hours per butterfly.org.au; one older listing gives shorter hours).
- In a crisis: **000** or **Lifeline 13 11 14**.
- Binge eating disorder responds well to treatment. Asking early is sensible, not dramatic.

## Gaps and cautions
- **No study matches Kye's exact pattern.** Every number for a single day comes from 24-h to 14-day chamber studies, mostly in untrained men and women, scaled by arithmetic.
- The fraction stored is well measured, at 75–95 %. The split between glycogen and fat on day 1 in a person in a deficit is **not measured**. The 30–70 g "immediate fat" estimate for a carb-heavy day is my inference (UNVERIFIED).
- **Very limited:** no single-day salt study was found, and no measurement of the weight of gut contents after a big day.
- The protein-overfeeding evidence comes from one lab, is self-reported and small (Antonio). Bray 2012 is the controlled anchor.
- No PMC full text was read (host blocked). Co-author lists beyond those shown in search results are flagged UNVERIFIED in the CSV.
