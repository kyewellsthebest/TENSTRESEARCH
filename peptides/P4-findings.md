# P4 findings: cognitive, longevity, sleep, tanning and sexual peptides

Agent P4. Searched 2026-11-04. 30 WebSearches; one WebFetch attempt on pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov failed (host not in this
session's network allowlist), so **no primary paper was read in full**. Every source below is ABSTRACT or SECONDARY,
and I say which. Nothing here is a dosing protocol. The only doses quoted are trial doses of approved drugs, given as
context. No sourcing information is given for any substance.

## 10-line summary

1. Only four substances in this group have a regulator-approved use backed by phase-3 RCTs: **bremelanotide** (Vyleesi,
   FDA 2019, low libido in premenopausal women), **afamelanotide** (Scenesse; FDA, EMA and TGA 2020, for a rare light-sensitivity disease called EPP),
   **elamipretide** (Forzinity, FDA *accelerated* approval Sept 2025, Barth syndrome) and **oxytocin** (obstetric use;
   the intranasal "social" uses are not approved).
2. **Cerebrolysin** has the largest trial volume (Cochrane: 7 RCTs, 1,773 people in acute stroke) and shows **no effect
   on death and a possible increase in serious adverse events**. Its positive CARS trials were small and funded by the manufacturer.
3. **Bremelanotide's benefit is real but small.** It raised the desire score by about 0.3 points over placebo. It did not
   increase the number of satisfying sexual events. Nausea hit 40% of users (1% on placebo).
4. **Elamipretide failed its large trial.** MMPOWER-3 (n=218) found no benefit in mitochondrial myopathy. Its Barth approval rests on a
   12-patient crossover trial that missed its primary endpoint, plus open-label and natural-history data. A confirmatory trial is required.
5. **Semax and Selank** are approved only in Russia. The human evidence is small Russian studies, mostly open-label or
   with no placebo. There are no independent Western RCTs.
6. **Epitalon, dihexa, humanin and MOTS-c have no placebo-controlled human efficacy data at all.** The only human data
   in this group is a company-reported phase 1 of a MOTS-c analogue (CB4211). Dihexa adds a theoretical cancer
   concern through its mechanism (HGF/c-Met).
7. **DSIP's** small 1980s–90s double-blind studies conflict, and the authors of the better-designed ones called the effect clinically
   insignificant.
8. **Melanotan II** has no approval anywhere. Its harms are known from case reports: new and changing moles, melanomas
   diagnosed during use, priapism, rhabdomyolysis, renal infarction, and a reported brain-swelling syndrome (PRES).
   Grey-market vials held 43–88% of the labelled dose plus 4–6% unknown impurities.
9. **Intranasal oxytocin** failed its largest RCT, in autism (n=290, null). **VIP (aviptadil)** failed a 461-patient COVID RCT
   and was stopped for futility. **Glutathione** has small oral RCTs. IV "skin-lightening" use has no RCT support and has
   drawn regulator warnings.
10. Nothing in this group has evidence for muscle, strength, physique or training recovery in healthy people. The
    substances with the most aggressive "peptide clinic" marketing (epitalon, dihexa, Semax, Selank, MT-II, MOTS-c) are
    the ones with the least human data.

Evidence levels are coded as RCT / NRS (non-randomised human) / CASE / ANIMAL / INVITRO / ANECDOTE.

## Cognitive

### Semax (ACTH(4-10)-derived heptapeptide)
- **Human benefit:** NRS/very limited. Gusev 1997 (PMID 11517472): 30 acute hemispheric stroke patients. The authors
  report "some influence on the rate of recovery". ABSTRACT. Gusev/Martynov group 2018: 110 post-stroke patients
  with early or late rehabilitation, with or without Semax; outcomes were plasma BDNF, motor scores and Barthel index. Secondary sources
  describe it as non-randomised and not placebo-controlled. I could not confirm the design from the abstract, and the effect sizes are not
  available. SECONDARY. **No RCT in healthy people for cognition has been found.**
- **Adverse events:** No systematic AE data was found. Unknown.
- **Unknowns:** Long-term safety, effects in healthy users, and whether it raises BDNF in people (shown in animals).
- **Regulatory:** Approved in Russia (and some CIS states). It was on the FDA 503A **Category 2** list (Sept 2023), and PCAC
  review was scheduled for July 2026 (outcome not found). TGA: not ARTG-registered; its Poisons Standard entry was not verified.
  WADA: not named. S0 ("no approval by any governmental health authority") arguably does not reach a drug approved in
  Russia. **Unresolved; athletes should treat it as risky.**
- **Grey market:** Vanhee 2020 (Drug Test Anal, PMID 31667971) identified Semax and Selank in seized preparations and
  found them sold online as injectable powders and nasal sprays. No purity figures were found. ABSTRACT.

### Selank (tuftsin analogue)
- **Human benefit:** RCT?/very limited. Zozulia 2008 (PMID 18454096): 62 patients with generalised anxiety or neurasthenia,
  Selank (30) vs medazepam (32). The abstract reports mainly enkephalin changes. Secondary sources say it was open-label
  with an active comparator and no placebo, and its anxiety outcomes are described inconsistently. ABSTRACT.
- **Adverse events / unknowns:** No systematic AE data. No independent replication.
- **Regulatory:** Russia only (approval year unverified). It was on FDA Category 2 in 2023. Its 2026 removal status
  **conflicts between sources**. TGA: not ARTG-registered. WADA: as for Semax.
- **Grey market:** Same Vanhee 2020 seizure data as Semax.

### Cerebrolysin (porcine brain-derived peptide mixture, IV)
- **Human benefit, acute stroke:** RCT/moderate certainty of **no** mortality benefit. Cochrane 2023 update: 7 RCTs,
  1,773 participants (one trial used the Cerebrolysin-like agent Cortexin, n=272). It concluded Cerebrolysin "probably has no beneficial
  effect on preventing all-cause death", with **moderate-certainty evidence of a possible increase in non-fatal serious adverse
  events**. The 2016 version (6 RCTs, 1,501 people) reached the same verdict. Three included trials involved the
  manufacturer. ABSTRACT/SECONDARY.
- **Motor recovery (CARS):** Muresanu 2016, Stroke 47:151-9, n=208. Upper-limb ARAT at day 90 showed a Mann-Whitney effect size of
  0.71 (95% CI 0.63–0.79) favouring Cerebrolysin. The authors called the study exploratory and small. **It was funded by EVER Neuro Pharma,
  and the lead author disclosed ties to the company.** The CARS-1+2 pooled analysis (Neurol Sci 2017, n=448) was by the same sponsor network. ABSTRACT.
- **Alzheimer's:** Gauthier 2015 meta-analysis, 6 RCTs. Cognition SMD -0.40 at 4 weeks, **not significant at 6 months**.
  ABSTRACT.
- **Adverse events:** Similar to placebo in individual trials, but Cochrane flags more serious AEs. I could not verify
  anaphylaxis or seizure frequencies in this session.
- **Regulatory:** Approved in roughly 50 countries (Austria, Russia, China and others; the count is unverified), but **not FDA-approved
  and not ARTG-registered** (unverified). WADA: not named.
- **Grey market:** No data found. It is an animal-derived biologic, so contamination risk is plausible but unmeasured.

### Dihexa (angiotensin IV analogue, oral small peptide)
- **Human benefit:** **None. No human trials.** Animal data only (Benoist 2014, JPET; rat cognition and HGF/c-Met
  potentiation). Wiki and vendor sites claim the foundational papers were **retracted in April 2025. UNVERIFIED**: I found
  no retraction notice.
- **Related human data:** Athira's fosgonimeton is a different HGF-modulating compound, not dihexa. It **failed** the phase
  2/3 LIFT-AD trial in Alzheimer's (~312 participants, Sept 2024). SECONDARY.
- **Serious unknown:** It potentiates HGF/c-Met, a pathway that drives tumour growth and metastasis. No carcinogenicity
  studies exist. ANIMAL/INVITRO reasoning only.
- **Regulatory:** FDA Category 2 in 2023, with PCAC review "before February 2027". TGA: not registered. WADA: S0 (no approval
  anywhere).

## Sleep

### DSIP (delta sleep-inducing peptide, emideltide)
- **Human benefit:** RCT/very limited and conflicting. Schneider-Helmert 1987 (PMID 3622582): 14 chronic insomniacs
  over 7 nights, double-blind; the author claimed benefit. Same year (PMID 3583493): improvement "of little clinical significance".
  1992 (PMID 1299794): 16 insomniacs, better sleep efficiency and latency vs placebo, but "not likely to be of major
  therapeutic benefit". All of these studies gave DSIP intravenously. ABSTRACT.
- **Adverse events:** No systematic data. **Unknowns:** Nearly everything, including whether it is a true endogenous sleep factor.
- **Regulatory:** FDA Category 2 in 2023. FDA says it was removed in 2026 because its nominations were withdrawn, which **does not make it
  compoundable**. PCAC review was July 2026, outcome not found. TGA: not registered. WADA: S0.

## Longevity

### Epitalon / epithalon (AEDG tetrapeptide; Khavinson)
- **Human benefit:** **No placebo-controlled human RCT found.** Khavinson's human programme was non-randomised and
  non-blinded, ran for 6–8 years in Russia, and much of it used the pineal *extract* epithalamin rather than the synthetic peptide. The telomerase claim
  rests on in-vitro work from the same lab, plus one reported independent in-vitro replication (2025). The review I relied on
  is a 2026 preprint (preprints.org 202609.0801) that has not been peer reviewed. SECONDARY.
- **Quality of evidence:** Very low. It comes from a single group, much of it in Russian-language journals, with no Western replication in
  humans. No human study has measured telomere length before and after treatment against placebo.
- **Unknowns:** Activating telomerase is a theoretical cancer-promotion concern. It is unmeasured.
- **Regulatory:** FDA Category 2 in 2023. FDA says it was removed in 2026 because its nominations were withdrawn. PCAC review was July 2026,
  outcome not found. TGA: not registered. WADA: S0.

### SS-31 / elamipretide (Forzinity)
- **Barth syndrome approval:** FDA **accelerated approval on 19 Sept 2025**, after a Complete Response Letter in May 2025. It is
  indicated for patients of at least 30 kg; the trial and label dose of 40 mg SC daily is given as context only. Approval rests on TAZPOWER
  knee-extensor strength as a surrogate. **A confirmatory RCT is required.** The FDA press release lists mild-to-moderate injection-site reactions as the most common AE.
  TAZPOWER itself was a 28-week crossover in **12 patients** and **missed its primary and secondary endpoints** in the
  blinded 12-week phase. The gains came in the open-label extension (stroke volume +27% at week 36). A natural-history
  comparison (Hornby 2022, Orphanet J Rare Dis) of 8 OLE patients against 19 untreated controls reported a 6MWT
  difference of +79.7 m at week 64. That design is weak because it has no randomisation. Sponsor: Stealth BioTherapeutics. SECONDARY.
- **Primary mitochondrial myopathy:** MMPOWER-3 (Karaa 2023, Neurology, PMID 37268435): RCT, n=218. 6MWT difference
  -3.2 m (95% CI -18.7 to 12.3). The fatigue score was also null. **A clear negative.** It was well tolerated. ABSTRACT.
- **Unknowns:** Long-term effects in healthy users, and any benefit outside rare mitochondrial disease. No exercise-performance RCT
  in healthy people was found.
- **Regulatory:** FDA-approved (accelerated) for Barth only. TGA: not ARTG-registered (unverified). WADA: not named.
  Grey-market "SS-31" made outside the approved product has no quality data.

### Humanin
- **Human benefit:** None. **No human interventional trial found.** Animal and cell data only.
- **Regulatory:** Unapproved. FDA category not verified. WADA: S0.

### MOTS-c (brief; P1 may cover it in more depth)
- **Human data:** Only CB4211, a MOTS-c *analogue* from CohBar. Phase 1a/1b was an RCT: 65 healthy adults, then 20 adults with obesity and
  NAFLD for 4 weeks. **The source is a company press release.** It reported falls in ALT/AST and glucose and no serious AEs, with injection-site reactions
  most common. The trial was **suspended in 2018 over injection-site reactions**. No phase 2 followed. SECONDARY.
  **No human trial of native MOTS-c.**
- **Regulatory:** Unapproved. WADA: S0. FDA Category 2 status not verified in this session.

### Excluded
NAD+ is not a peptide and is excluded, as the brief directs.

## Tanning

### Melanotan I → afamelanotide (Scenesse)
- **Human benefit:** RCT/moderate, in **EPP only**. Langendonk 2015 NEJM reported two RCTs (US 6 months, EU 9 months). Pain-free direct
  sunlight exposure was significantly longer in both, and quality of life improved. A review cites a median of +28.6 h pain-free
  exposure over 180 days. A company release on the earlier US phase III reported 64.1 h vs 47.5 h, p=0.107 (not significant). The
  primary endpoint changed between trials, which is worth noting. ABSTRACT/SECONDARY. **Sponsor: Clinuvel (Australian).**
- **Adverse events:** Not extracted in this session. Expected effects are nausea, headache, implant-site reactions and darkening of
  moles (from prior knowledge, not verified here).
- **Regulatory:** FDA 2019 (not re-verified), EMA, and **TGA October 2020** (ARTG; adults; priority review;
  additional monitoring). It is a controlled-release implant, and **there is no approved cosmetic tanning use**. WADA: not named.

### Melanotan II
- **Human benefit:** No approved indication. Small early trials in erectile dysfunction exist but were not verified this session. **No
  RCT for tanning.**
- **Adverse events (CASE level):** New and changing naevi. Melanomas diagnosed during use, including melanoma in situ 4 weeks
  after starting (2021 case report; author name inconsistent across sources, UNVERIFIED). Priapism needing urological
  intervention. Rhabdomyolysis with renal dysfunction (MT-II confirmed by mass spectrometry). Renal infarction (Peters 2020).
  The TGA also lists brain swelling. The UK MHRA received 16 spontaneous suspected-ADR reports in 2012–2022 (FOI 24/274). Case
  reports cannot establish frequency or causation. **The melanoma link is not proven, but the mechanism (MC1R
  stimulation driving naevus proliferation) makes it a live concern.**
- **Grey market:** Breindahl 2015 (Drug Test Anal) tested vials labelled 10 mg and found **4.32–8.84 mg**, with unknown impurities of
  4.1–5.9% in products from two of three shops. ABSTRACT.
- **Regulatory:** TGA: prescription-only, with no ARTG product. Supply without a prescription is illegal, and in 2026 an individual paid 27 infringement notices
  totalling A$101,412. FDA: unapproved; Category 2 in 2023, removed in 2026 after nominations were withdrawn (still not compoundable),
  with PCAC review before Feb 2027. WADA: not named; falls under S0.

## Sexual

### Bremelanotide / PT-141 (Vyleesi)
- **Human benefit:** RCT/moderate. RECONNECT 301 and 302 (Kingsberg 2019, Obstet Gynecol 134:899-908) pooled
  ~1,267 premenopausal women with HSDD, testing the trial and label dose of 1.75 mg SC as needed. On the FSFI desire domain, change was
  0.54 vs 0.24 (301) and 0.63 vs 0.21 (302). The integrated distress-score change was -0.33, effect size 0.27. **There was no increase in
  satisfying sexual events**, and an editorial called the clinical benefit "hard to gauge". A 52-week open-label extension
  (Simon 2019) showed no new safety signals. **Sponsor: Palatin/AMAG.** SECONDARY.
- **Adverse events:** Nausea 40% vs 1% (median duration 2.4 h), flushing 20% vs 0.3%, headache 11% vs 2%. Blood pressure rose transiently by
  about +3/+2 mmHg, and the drug is contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension or cardiovascular disease. Focal hyperpigmentation is on
  the label (prior knowledge, not re-verified).
- **Unknowns:** Use in men, postmenopausal women, or at grey-market doses.
- **Regulatory:** FDA approved June 2019. TGA: not ARTG-registered (not verified). WADA: not named; it is approved, so S0
  does not apply. **Grey-market "PT-141"** comes with vendor certificates of analysis only, and I found no independent testing.

### Oxytocin (intranasal)
- **Human benefit for social or cognitive uses:** It failed the largest RCT. Sikich 2021 NEJM: 290 children and adolescents with autism over 24
  weeks. The social-withdrawal score changed -3.7 on oxytocin vs -3.5 on placebo (P=0.61), and secondary outcomes were null. Earlier positive studies were small and
  underpowered. SECONDARY. The wider "trust and bonding" literature has replication problems, but I did not survey it here.
- **Adverse events:** Similar to placebo in Sikich. There was one serious event, a sedation-related car accident in a 17-year-old.
- **Regulatory:** Approved as an injection for obstetric use (FDA; TGA, with ARTG status for injection assumed and unverified). Intranasal
  social or cognitive use is unapproved everywhere I checked. WADA: not named.

### VIP (vasoactive intestinal peptide, aviptadil). Brief
- **Human benefit:** RCT/none for its tested indication. TESICO (Brown 2023, Lancet Respir Med, doi
  10.1016/S2213-2600(23)00147-9): 461 people hospitalised with COVID respiratory failure. It was **stopped for futility**, with an
  ordinal OR of 1.40 (95% CI 0.94–2.08). The day-5 safety composite occurred in 63% on aviptadil vs 56% on placebo. ABSTRACT. An aviptadil/phentolamine
  combination for erectile dysfunction is approved in some European countries (UNVERIFIED).
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved. TGA: not registered (unverified). WADA: not named.

### Glutathione (tripeptide). Brief
- **Oral:** Richie 2015 (Eur J Nutr, PMID 24791752): an RCT in 54 healthy adults over 6 months. Secondary sources report dose-dependent
  rises in body stores, but other RCTs conflict. There are 3 small oral RCTs for skin lightening. SECONDARY.
- **IV for skin lightening:** **No RCT efficacy evidence.** The Philippine FDA warned against it, citing liver dysfunction in 32%
  of patients in one study and anaphylaxis. A reported US FDA alert against compounding injectables from
  dietary-supplement-grade powder is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Regulatory:** An oral supplement ingredient in many countries. The injection is unapproved for cosmetic use. **WADA:
  glutathione is not prohibited, but IV infusions over 100 mL per 12 hours are banned (M2.2)** outside hospital or clinical
  investigation (from the WADA list; not re-read this session).

## Cross-cutting flags

- **COI.** CARS and the CARS-1+2 pooled analysis were funded by EVER Neuro Pharma. RECONNECT was run by Palatin/AMAG, elamipretide by Stealth, afamelanotide by
  Clinuvel, and CB4211 by CohBar (data from a press release only). The Semax, Selank and epitalon literature comes from its originating
  Russian groups. Many of the "reviews" returned by search are peptide-vendor or "peptide clinic" pages. They have an
  obvious commercial interest and were used only to find primary records, never as evidence.
- **FDA 503A, 2026.** Several substances, including epitalon, DSIP and MT-II, were dropped from Category 2 because their nominations were
  withdrawn. FDA states that this **does not make them eligible for compounding**. The July 2026 PCAC outcomes were not
  found. Check the current FDA bulks list.
- **Grey-market data for this group:** MT-II (Breindahl 2015; underdosed, with impurities). Semax and Selank found in seizures (Vanhee
  2020). Iceland customs (July 2026) could not confirm the labelled active ingredient in "a large proportion" of seized peptide samples,
  and found significant impurities. In Norway's 2011–14 seizures, peptides and proteins made up 28% of preparations. Endotoxin and
  sterility data for these specific peptides were not found.
- **Relevance to Muscle Band:** None of these substances has demonstrated an effect on muscle fatigue, strength or
  training outcomes in healthy people. The one muscle-strength signal (elamipretide) comes from Barth syndrome, a rare
  mitochondrial disease, in 12 patients.
