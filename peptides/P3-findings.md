# P3 — Healing / recovery / skin / immune peptides

Agent P3, 2026-11-04. Brief: `BRIEF.md`. Studies table: `P3-studies.csv`; scores: `P3-scores.csv`; searches: `P3-searchlog.md`.
**Access caveat:** PMC (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) does not resolve from this environment (one WebFetch attempt failed), so no full text
was read. Every number here comes from abstracts quoted in search snippets (ABSTRACT) or from reviews, trade press and press releases
(SECONDARY). Anything not confirmed is marked UNVERIFIED. No dosing, cycling, injection or sourcing information is given, by design.

## Plain-English summary (10 lines)

1. **BPC-157, the most hyped peptide here, has no published controlled human trial.** The human record is 30 people in three uncontrolled clinic reports plus two old enema trials that were only ever published as conference abstracts.
2. Those three reports come from one US clinic owner whose clinic sold the injections. Of the >190 animal papers, more than 80% come from a single group in Zagreb.
3. TB-500 is **not** thymosin beta-4. It is a 7-amino-acid fragment that has **zero** human studies. The ~1,700 people in thymosin beta-4 trials received the full 43-aa protein, mostly as eye drops or a gel.
4. Full-length thymosin beta-4 has a failed phase 3 programme: eye drops for dry eye missed their primary endpoints. Wound gels were safe but no better than placebo.
5. Thymosin alpha-1 is the only drug here approved anywhere (hepatitis B, in >35 countries). Its largest trial (1,106 sepsis patients, BMJ 2025) showed **no** survival benefit, and it has never been tested for recovery or performance in healthy people.
6. LL-37 failed its main 148-patient leg-ulcer trial. ARA-290 has three small nerve-fibre trials on surrogate endpoints, and its developer has reportedly closed. KPV has no human data at all.
7. Copper peptide (GHK-Cu) has a few small trials of **skin cream**, with mixed results. No study of any kind exists for injecting it.
8. Collagen peptides (a food) are the only item here with many RCTs. With lifting, they add a small amount of fat-free mass (SMD ~0.5) and give modest knee-OA relief. Almost all the muscle trials are linked to one manufacturer (Gelita). Skin benefits disappeared when only independent trials were counted (2025 meta-analysis, contested).
9. Safety: no serious harm shows up in the trials, but the trials are too small and short to detect one. Pro-angiogenic biology (BPC-157, Tβ4) creates a theoretical cancer-growth concern that nobody has tested. Grey-market vials vary by up to ±80% in content, and HPLC testing does not check sterility.
10. Regulation (Nov 2026): BPC-157 and TB-500 are prescription-only plus Appendix D in Australia and banned by WADA (S0 / S2). An FDA advisory panel voted 8-6 in July 2026 to allow US compounding of BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV over unanimous staff objection. That vote is advisory, and the final FDA action was not found.

## Ranking (this agent's nine substances)

**(a) Research volume, most to least:**
collagen peptides > thymosin alpha-1 > thymosin beta-4 (full length) > LL-37 > ARA-290 > GHK-Cu (topical) > BPC-157 > TB-500 fragment = KPV (zero).

**(b) Demonstrated benefit, best to worst:**
1. Collagen + resistance training: small FFM gain; modest OA effect (moderate/low-moderate).
2. Thymosin alpha-1: hepatitis B response (weak, sponsor-pooled). Nothing in healthy people.
3. GHK-Cu topical: wrinkles (one small trial).
4. ARA-290: surrogate nerve endpoints.
5. Thymosin beta-4: one dry-eye symptom.
6. LL-37: post-hoc subgroup only.
7. BPC-157: uncontrolled reports only.
8. TB-500 and KPV: nothing.

**(c) Side effects, best-characterised/mildest to least known:**
1. Collagen: GI only; very large exposure as a food.
2. Thymosin alpha-1: well tolerated across thousands, incl. a 1,106-patient RCT.
3. Thymosin beta-4: mild in phase 1 (40) and eye-drop trials (~1,500).
4. LL-37 topical (~150).
5. ARA-290 (~120, not characterised in what was read).
6. GHK-Cu: topical known; injected unknown.
7. BPC-157: n≈30 uncontrolled; theoretical angiogenesis/tumour concern; grey-market variability.
8. TB-500 and KPV: no human safety data whatsoever.

## BPC-157 (pentadecapeptide; PL 14736 / PL-10 / Bepecin)

**Every human study found:**

| Study | Design | n | What it showed | Access |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PL 14736 phase I, c.2002-03 (abstract) | placebo-controlled, rectal | 32 healthy men (per Croatian bibliography listing) | no significant AEs | SECONDARY |
| PL 14736 phase II ulcerative colitis, Gastroenterology 2005 abstract | described as multicentre DB placebo-controlled | not found | "effective and safe" per a secondary review; **no data located**; never published in full; development stopped | SECONDARY, UNVERIFIED |
| Lee & Padgett 2021, Altern Ther Health Med, PMID 34324435 | retrospective chart/phone review | 16 (12 BPC-only, 4 BPC+TB4) | reported 11/12 and 3/4 "improved"; no validated scale; one review reports 7/12, a discrepancy left unresolved | SECONDARY |
| Lee, Walker, Ayadi 2024, Altern Ther Health Med 30(10):12-17, PMID 39325560 | uncontrolled pilot, intravesical injection at cystoscopy | 12 women with interstitial cystitis | aggregator reports 10/12 resolution (UNVERIFIED) | SECONDARY |
| Lee & Burgess 2025, Altern Ther Health Med, PMID 40131143 | uncontrolled IV safety pilot | **2** | no biomarker change, no AEs | SECONDARY |

- **Demonstrated human benefit:** none. Evidence level: human non-randomised (uncontrolled) only, very limited. A secondary blog cites a 2026 review counting "three published human studies, <30 participants". That fits the table, but the review itself was not reached.
- **COI:** Edwin Lee is first author on all three modern reports and runs the Institute for Hormonal Balance (Orlando), the clinic where patients paid for the injections. This is a peptide-clinic conflict of interest of exactly the type the brief requires flagged.
- **Animal literature:** large but concentrated. A 2025 Pharmaceuticals (MDPI 18(10):1451) exchange reports a PubMed search (20 May 2025) of >190 "BPC 157" articles, of which >80% list Sikiric or a co-author (SECONDARY). Independent replication is sparse; a Taiwanese group (Chang et al., 2014) is cited as one independent source. The Vasireddi 2025 HSS J systematic review (PMID 40756949) found 36 studies (35 preclinical, 1 clinical). Its findings: animal half-life <30 min, no lethal dose in four preclinical safety studies, and "we caution against the use of BPC-157 by clinicians and athletes."
- **Adverse events:** none recorded in n≈30, which means nothing at that size.
- **Unknowns:**
  - Long-term safety.
  - Immunogenicity, which was the FDA's stated concern.
  - **Angiogenesis and cancer.** Preclinical work shows VEGFR2 → Akt–eNOS / ERK1/2 activation, which raises a theoretical concern about supporting tumour vascularisation. No human tumour data exist in either direction. Some preclinical reports claim anti-tumour effects. The honest status is untested, not "safe".
- **Regulatory:**
  - **TGA:** Schedule 4 plus Appendix D. Not on the ARTG. Cannot come in under the Personal Importation Scheme. The June 2024 listing date comes from secondary sources and is not verified against the TGA document.
  - **WADA:** S0, named as an example of non-approved substances (seen in national annexes for 2026).
  - **FDA:**
    - Placed on Category 2 in Sept 2023, for immunogenicity, impurities and insufficient safety data.
    - Reportedly removed from Category 2 in April 2026. Sources give 15 or 23 April.
    - PCAC voted **8-6 with one abstention on 23 July 2026** to recommend BPC-157, KPV and TB-500 for the 503A bulks list. FDA staff scientists unanimously opposed it.
    - The vote is advisory, and rulemaking is needed. No final FDA action was found. Commentary notes scrutiny of conflicts of interest among newly appointed committee members.
- **Grey-market quality:**
  - A Finnrick dataset (469 BPC-157 samples, 69 vendors, per secondary summary): purity typically 96.25-99.95% (5th-95th percentile), but **quantity up to ±80% off label** (95th percentile).
  - An April 2026 preprint analysing 6,441 Finnrick samples across 14 peptides was not read for its BPC-157 result.
  - HPLC does not test endotoxin, sterility or heavy metals.
  - The often-quoted "7.7% purity, 100% endotoxin" figures are from a **semaglutide** study and must not be attributed to BPC-157.
  - Reports of wrong-peptide samples are anecdotal.

## TB-500 vs thymosin beta-4 (Tβ4)

- **TB-500 is a different molecule.** Ghent researchers (Drug Test Anal 2012, doi 10.1002/dta.1402) identified the active ingredient of marketed "TB-500" as **N-acetyl-LKKTETQ**, the 17-23 fragment of Tβ4. It has been detected in racehorses. **Human studies of the fragment: zero.** No Tβ4 trial result below can be transferred to it.
- **Full-length Tβ4 human trials:**
  - **IV phase 1**, Ann N Y Acad Sci 2010;1194:223-9 (PMID 20536472). 40 healthy volunteers, single and 14-day dosing. AEs infrequent, mild-moderate; no SAE or dose-limiting toxicity.
  - **Recombinant NL005 phase 1** (China, n=54). Results not read.
  - **RGN-259 eye drops (dry eye):**
    - ARISE-1/2/3, ~1,500 patients pooled. ARISE-2 ~600, per the sponsor.
    - ARISE-3 **missed its pre-specified co-primary endpoints**. It showed significant improvement in ocular grittiness at 1-2 weeks; the corneal-staining benefit was in a post-hoc subgroup only.
    - The sponsor's pooled analysis is positive but post hoc.
    - SEER-1 (neurotrophic keratopathy, phase 3): no efficacy result found.
    - Consistently safe.
  - **Topical gel, venous stasis ulcers** (NCT00832091, n=72): not significant vs placebo on complete healing or time to healing.
  - **Pressure ulcers** (n=72): safe; no efficacy result found.
  - **Cardiac:** the phase 1 recommended development for cardiac ischaemia. No human efficacy trial in MI was found.
- **Demonstrated benefit:** one dry-eye symptom, short-term. Evidence level: human RCTs, weak. None for musculoskeletal healing.
- **AEs:** mild and infrequent across ~1,700.
- **Unknowns:** systemic long-term use. Tβ4 promotes cell migration and angiogenesis and is studied in tumour biology, so a theoretical concern exists; it is untested in humans.
- **Regulatory:**
  - **TGA:** a delegate's interim decision placed Tβ4, TB-500 and fibroblast growth factors in Schedule 4 plus Appendix D (implementation proposed 1 June 2016). The final decision was not confirmed.
  - **WADA:** S2 (Tβ4 and its derivatives, e.g. TB-500), per national annexes. One site says S0. Defer to the official list.
  - **FDA:** TB-500 was on Category 2 and was removed in 2026. The PCAC voted in favour in July 2026; not final.
- **Grey market:** no TB-500-specific purity dataset found.

## Thymosin alpha-1 (thymalfasin, Zadaxin)

- **Approved:** for chronic hepatitis B in >35 countries, including China and Italy (secondary sources). Not FDA-approved.
- **Biggest RCT:** TESTS, BMJ 2025 (PMID 39814420). 1,106 adults with sepsis at 22 Chinese centres. 28-day mortality 23.4% vs 24.1%, **HR 0.99 (0.77-1.27), P=0.93**. The diabetic subgroup (interaction P=0.04) is exploratory. Safety good.
- **Earlier evidence:** a 2025 Frontiers meta-analysis pooled the smaller trials to OR 0.73 (0.59-0.90), but its own trial-sequential analysis says the evidence is insufficient. The large trial overrides it.
- **Hepatitis B:** a sponsor pooled analysis reported 38% vs 21% response, and a Taiwan trial missed significance. Meta-analysis citations (5 RCTs, 653 patients) conflict between sources and are UNVERIFIED.
- **COVID:** one retrospective cohort (n=3,240) is cited; UNVERIFIED and observational.
- **For gym/recovery use:** no study in healthy or training people. "Immune optimisation" claims are extrapolation.
- **AEs:** well tolerated in thousands.
- **Unknowns:** effects in autoimmunity, and in healthy people.
- **Regulatory:**
  - **TGA:** not found.
  - **WADA:** not found named (UNVERIFIED).
  - **FDA:** Category 2 (2023), removed Sept 2024 after the nomination was withdrawn. Reportedly not recommended for Category 1.
- **Grey market:** no data found.

## GHK-Cu (copper tripeptide)

- **Topical:**
  - Badenhorst 2016 (J Aging Sci), RCT, n=40 women aged 40-65, 8 weeks, nano-carrier formulation. Wrinkle volume −55.8% (p<0.001) and depth −32.8% (p=0.012) vs vehicle.
  - A post-CO2-laser study found **no objective improvement**, only higher patient satisfaction.
  - The often-cited Leyden/Abdulghani photoaging trials were not reached. A claimed "2025 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (n=456)" appears only on vendor sites and is UNVERIFIED.
- **Injected:** **no human trials at all.** Injection-site reactions are reported only anecdotally on vendor sites. Systemic copper load and immunogenicity are unstudied.
- **Regulatory:**
  - **TGA:** not specifically scheduled (secondary, UNVERIFIED).
  - **WADA:** not named.
  - **FDA:** injectable GHK-Cu was on Category 2 and was removed in 2026 after the nomination was withdrawn. Removal does not make it compoundable; a PCAC review is planned before the end of February 2027.

## KPV (Lys-Pro-Val)

- **Human trials:** none. The evidence is mouse colitis (Kannengiesser 2008, Inflamm Bowel Dis 14(3):324) and cell work (Dalmasso 2008: uptake via PepT1, NF-κB reduction).
- **Unsupported claim:** a vendor claim of a 24-patient UC trial (58% vs 15% remission) has no publication or registry entry and should be treated as unsupported.
- **Safety:** no human toxicology.
- **Regulatory:**
  - **FDA:** the PCAC voted in July 2026 to recommend it for compounding; not final.
  - **TGA:** not found.
  - **WADA:** not named; S0 would plausibly apply (interpretation).

## LL-37 (ropocamptide)

- **HEAL LL-37 phase IIb** (Wound Repair Regen 2021;29(6):938-950, PMID 34687253): n=148, hard-to-heal venous leg ulcers, 13 weeks, topical. **No significant healing benefit** in the full population. The sponsor (Promore Pharma, which funded the trial) highlights a large-ulcer subgroup. Safe.
- **Earlier work:** a phase I/II trial (Grönberg 2014) was not found.
- **Injected use:** never trialled. LL-37 is implicated in psoriasis and lupus inflammation, so a theoretical concern exists.
- **Regulatory:**
  - **FDA:** Category 2, removed in 2026 (nomination withdrawn).
  - **TGA:** not found.
  - **WADA:** not named.

## ARA-290 (cibinetide)

EPO-derived 11-aa peptide that acts on the innate repair receptor and does not stimulate red-cell production.

- **RCTs, all in sarcoidosis small-fibre neuropathy:**
  - Heij 2012: n=22, IV, 4 weeks.
  - Dahan 2013 (Mol Med, PMID 24136731): ~36, 28 days. Better symptoms, corneal nerve density and 6-minute walk.
  - Culver 2017 (IOVS): phase 2b, n=64. Corneal nerve fibre area increased.
  - Effect sizes not read.
- **Gaps:** a type-2 diabetes trial (Brines 2015) was not reached. There is no phase 3. The developer, Araim, has reportedly closed.
- **Benefit:** surrogate endpoints in a rare disease. Nothing for training or recovery.
- **Regulatory:** not approved. TGA not found. WADA status ambiguous (S0 vs S2); check with the national anti-doping organisation.

## Collagen peptides (oral, food)

**Muscle trials (all with resistance training 3×/week, 15 g/day, 12 weeks):**

| Trial | Population | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Zdzieblik 2015 (Br J Nutr 114:1237) | 53 sarcopenic men, mean age 72 | FFM +4.2 vs +2.9 kg; quadriceps +16.5 vs +7.3 Nm; FM −5.4 vs −3.5 kg (all P<0.05) |
| Jendricke 2019 (Nutrients 11:892) | 77 premenopausal women | greater FFM and grip gain (p<0.05) |
| Kirmse 2019 (Nutrients 11:1154) | 57 young men | greater FFM (p<0.05); strength not different; **fibre CSA rose equally in both groups**, so the authors suggest the extra FFM may be connective tissue, not contractile muscle |

**Meta-analyses:**
- **Training (Sports Med 2024;54:2865):** 19 studies, 768 participants. FFM SMD 0.48; small strength effect; low-moderate certainty.
- **Network meta-analysis (2026):** ranks collagen above whey. Treat this as implausible-until-replicated: it rests on indirect comparisons, and the collagen trials largely used non-protein placebos.
- **Osteoarthritis:**
  - García-Coronado 2019: WOMAC total −8.0 (−13.0 to −3.0), but the pain subscale was null.
  - 2023 JOSR meta-analysis: pain SMD −0.58 (−0.98 to −0.18), I² 68%.
- **Skin:**
  - Myung 2025 (Am J Med): 23 RCTs, 1,474 participants. Positive overall, but the **non-industry-funded and high-quality subgroups showed no effect**. Industry bodies dispute the funding classification.
  - A 2023 Nutrients meta-analysis (26 RCTs, 1,721 participants) was positive.

**COI:** Zdzieblik was partly funded by Gelita, which makes the product. The Jendricke full text is hosted by Gelita, and Kirmse and Oertzen-Hagemann come from the same programme. The muscle evidence is largely one manufacturer's research programme.

**Other points:**
- **AEs:** GI only. **Regulatory:** food; not WADA-prohibited.
- **Context for Kye:** the comparator that matters is extra protein, not placebo. Whether collagen beats an equal-protein control is not established by these trials.
