# P5 — Cross-cutting safety, quality and regulation of peptides

Agent P5, 2026-11-04. Topic: grey-market quality, injection harms, Australian/US/WADA regulation, peptide clinics,
cancer and immunogenicity concerns, athlete cases, and a proposed scoring rubric for the three rankings.

**Access caveat that applies to everything below.** WebFetch could not reach the one page tried (raps.org was blocked

in this session.

## 10-line plain-English summary

1. Grey-market "research peptides" are often not what the label says. Independent tests have found purity of 5–75%, content that was wrong in both directions, arsenic and lead, endotoxin, and vials with no peptide in them at all.
2. Online semaglutide is the best-documented example: the products were 7–14% pure against a claimed 99%, carried 29–39% more drug than labelled, and contained endotoxin (Ashraf2024). That is a dosing risk as well as a contamination risk.
3. In Australia, BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, the GHRPs and MK-677 are Schedule 4 (prescription only) and listed in Appendix D. None of them is on the ARTG. Since 2026 the TGA has treated illegal peptides as a named compliance priority.
4. Real harm in Australia in 2026: Victoria recorded 6 acute liver injuries in people using a product labelled "retatrutide", and 13 anaphylaxis cases linked to unapproved peptides since July 2025.
5. In sport, BPC-157 is named under S0 (Non-Approved Substances), and the GH secretagogues, GHRH analogues, GHRPs, TB-500, AOD-9604 and MK-677 are all named under S2. They are banned at all times. Semaglutide and tirzepatide are not prohibited.
6. FDA status in the US is in motion. In 2023 the FDA put about 17 peptides in compounding "Category 2" (significant safety risk). Most have since come off it because the nominators withdrew, not because new safety data arrived. In July 2026 an advisory panel voted to recommend 6 of 7 peptides (including BPC-157) against its own staff's advice. Nothing becomes compoundable until rulemaking is finished.
7. "Peptide clinics" make money from what they prescribe, so their claims are not independent evidence. Australia fined one A$10M in 2019, and the court found no medical justification for the uses it advertised.
8. The cancer concern for growth-promoting peptides is theoretical, but it has a basis. Higher natural IGF-1 is modestly and probably causally linked to breast and colorectal cancer (HR around 1.1). For BPC-157's pro-angiogenic mechanism there are no human data either way.
9. Immunogenicity is real even for approved, pharmaceutical-grade peptides: tirzepatide raised anti-drug antibodies in 51% of trial patients, without loss of effect. Grey-market impurities and aggregates are the FDA's stated reason to expect worse.
10. For the rankings, an "unknown" side-effect profile must never score as "mild". The rubric below scores severity and how well the side effects are characterised as two separate axes.

## 1. Grey-market product quality (purity, contamination, mislabelling)

Evidence level: analytical (product-quality) studies. Strength: moderate that quality problems are common;
limited for any exact market-wide percentage.

| source | what was tested | finding | access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Janvier2018 (Talanta, Sciensano, Belgium) | 10 most common falsified peptide drugs from illegal internet pharmacies | Large variation in amount per unit; **purity 5–75%** (cysteine-containing peptides); **arsenic and lead**, several samples up to ~10x the ICH parenteral limit | SECONDARY |
| Ashraf2024 (JAMA Netw Open) | 6 online semaglutide orders, of which 3 arrived | All contained semaglutide; **purity 7–14%** vs 99% claimed; content **+29–39%** over label; one sample **endotoxin 8.95 EU/mg**; no viable microbes; 42% of 317 pharmacies judged illegal | SECONDARY |

It is not peer-reviewed, and the dataset is crowd- and vendor-submitted (selection bias).** | SECONDARY |

What this means:
- **"Not for human consumption / research use only" labelling** is how these products reach the market without
  being regulated as medicines (Rudge, The Conversation 2026; TGA alerts). That label is the reason no
  sterility, endotoxin or content control applies to them.
- **Vendor CoAs are not evidence.** They cover one lot, the seller commissions them, and different sellers give
  different molecular formulas for the same molecule. A high HPLC purity figure does not rule out endotoxin.
- **Wrong dose runs in both directions.** Underdosed products (purity 7–14%) and overdosed ones (+39%) were both
  found. For a drug with a narrow therapeutic window, such as a GLP-1 agonist, an overdose is a direct harm.
- A fact-check rated the claim "online BPC-157 contains lead" mostly false: the lead data came from supplements,
  not BPC-157. The Belgian heavy-metal finding applies to falsified peptide medicines in general.

## 2. Injection and administration harms

Evidence level: case reports and public-health surveillance. Strength: limited. These show that the harms happen;
their frequency is unknown.

- **Non-tuberculous mycobacterial abscesses** after injected cosmetic peptides (argireline, *M. abscessus*,
  Chen2021), after self-administered growth hormone (GH_NTM_case2021), and after other IM injections. The suspected
  route was a contaminated drug, diluent or syringe. I found no case series that names a gym peptide (BPC-157,
  TB-500) as the cause of an abscess. That absence reflects missing reporting, not evidence of safety.
- **Anaphylaxis:** 13 cases in Victoria linked to unapproved peptides since July 2025, all but one in people with no
  previous anaphylaxis (VicHealth_anaph2026). An April 2026 TGA advisory reported severe allergic reactions, systemic
  inflammatory response syndrome and generalised hypersensitivity (SECONDARY).
- **Liver injury:** 6 acute liver injuries in Victorian users of a product labelled retatrutide
  (VicHealth_reta2026). Whether the cause is the drug, a contaminant or a different substance in the vial is not
  established in what I could see.
- **Melanotan II:** the case-report literature is dominated by eruptive melanocytic naevi, some later found
  malignant, plus a few cases of renal infarct or rhabdomyolysis and priapism needing intervention (BestBET_MT2).
  Causation for melanoma is not established, and frequencies are unknown.

## 3. Australia: TGA, ABF and the clinics

- **Scheduling.** The TGA's Poisons Standard page lists CJC-1295, ipamorelin, GHRP-6, GHRPs, GHRHs and other GH
  secretagogues in **Schedule 4 and Appendix D Part 5**. Ibutamoren (MK-677) is captured by the GHS entry. TGA
  safety alerts (secondary) state **BPC-157 is Schedule 4 and in Appendix D**. A legal article (LSJ) groups TB-500 with
  them. **Melanotan II has been Schedule 4 since 1 June 2018.** A vendor site's claim that it moved to Schedule 9 in
  February 2026 is **not supported** by any TGA source I found. None of these products is on the ARTG.
- **Personal importation.** The TGA says unapproved peptides that are not clearly labelled cannot be assessed
  under the Personal Importation Scheme and "will not be considered for release". An importer must also meet the
  scheme's other conditions, including a prescription where one is required. If those conditions are not met,
  importation may be a criminal offence (LSJ). Quantity limits differ between sources and were not verified.
- **Enforcement.**
  - *Secretary, Dept of Health v Peptide Clinics Australia* [2019] FCA 1107: an **A$10M penalty** for advertising
    S4 peptides. The court accepted that there was no medical justification for the advertised uses, and found the
    company obstructive.
  - Melanotan II supplier: 27 infringement notices, A$101,412, paid May 2026.
  - ABF and TGA, April 2026: 640 vials of PIEDs intercepted, 76 further consignments, three people charged, more
    than A$2M of steroids and peptides.
  - The TGA's 2026–27 compliance priorities name illegal peptides. Its June 2026 media release and safety alert to
    importers, compounders and suppliers name **BPC-157, GHK-Cu, TB-500, retatrutide, CJC-1295 and the "Wolverine
    stack"**.
- **The loophole.** Rudge (University of Sydney, The Conversation, August 2026) describes online clinics supplying
  BPC-157 and TB-500 as **compounded, patient-named** products. Each dose is "for a particular person", so it never
  goes through TGA pre-market assessment. The gap sits between the TGA, which regulates products, and Ahpra, which
  regulates prescribers. The Medical Board of Australia is writing guidance on off-label, unapproved and compounded
  peptide prescribing; its content and timing were not found. The TGA has said compounded BPC-157 carries
  "significant uncertainties".
- **GLP-1 compounding.** The TGA stopped pharmacy compounding of GLP-1 agonists from October 2024 (prior knowledge,
  not re-verified).

## 4. United States: FDA 503A Category 2, and what changed in 2024–2026

- **September 2023.** The FDA placed a group of nominated peptides in **Category 2**, meaning bulk substances that
  may present significant safety risks in compounding. The group included BPC-157, TB-500, AOD-9604, CJC-1295,
  ipamorelin, **injectable** GHK-Cu, KPV, MOTS-c, epitalon, emideltide (DSIP), selank, semax and thymosin alpha-1.
  Melanotan II, LL-37, dihexa and PEG-MGF also appear in 2026 lists. The stated grounds were immunogenicity for some
  routes, peptide-related impurities, difficulty characterising the API, aggregation, and a lack of safety data
  (FDA_Cat2_2023, SECONDARY).
- **2024.** AOD-9604, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, thymosin alpha-1 and selank came off Category 2 **because the nominators
  withdrew**, not because of any safety finding. They are not on the 503A bulks list.
- **April 2026.** The FDA announced that about 12 more would leave Category 2, also after withdrawals. Sources
  disagree on exactly which. The FDA stated that leaving Category 2 **does not make a substance compoundable** and
  does not place it in Category 1. Non-injectable GHK-Cu was reportedly put back in Category 1 in May 2026
  (secondary).
- **PCAC, 23–24 July 2026.** The panel recommended **six of seven** for the 503A bulks list: **BPC-157** (ulcerative
  colitis, 8–6–1), **KPV**, **TB-500**, **MOTS-c**, **epitalon** (7–4–1) and **semax** (8–5–1). It rejected
  **emideltide/DSIP** (6–7–1). FDA staff had recommended against all seven, citing "lack of evidence to support the
  effectiveness" of BPC-157 and safety concerns. The vote is advisory; the FDA still has to complete notice-and-
  comment rulemaking. GHK-Cu, LL-37, dihexa, PEG-MGF and melanotan II are reportedly due before February 2027. CSPI
  wrote to the FDA in October 2026 objecting (letter not read).
- **Reading.** The movement in 2024–2026 is procedural and political. None of it reflects new human safety or
  efficacy data, and a panel vote is not an approval.
- **Approved peptides relevant to body composition** (prior knowledge): semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide;
  tesamorelin (HIV-associated abdominal fat); bremelanotide (sexual desire, not body composition). Retatrutide is
  investigational.

## 5. WADA Prohibited List

- **S0 Non-Approved Substances.** This covers any pharmacological substance not addressed elsewhere on the List and
  without current approval from any government health authority. **BPC-157 is named explicitly** (2026 List). It is
  a Specified Substance with a default 2-year ban (USADA).
- **S2 Peptide hormones, growth factors, related substances and mimetics, prohibited at all times.**
  - GHRH and analogues: CJC-1293, CJC-1295, sermorelin, tesamorelin.
  - GH secretagogues: ipamorelin, **ibutamoren (MK-677)**, anamorelin, macimorelin and others.
  - GHRPs: GHRP-1 to GHRP-6, hexarelin.
  - "Thymosin-β4 and its derivatives e.g. **TB-500**".
  - GH fragments: **AOD-9604**, hGH 176-191.
  - (from the 2021–2025 Lists, read via federation copies)
- **Not prohibited:** semaglutide and tirzepatide. Semaglutide has been in the Monitoring Program since 2024, and a
  report on the 2027 List says GLP-1 agonists remain permitted.
- **Not named:** melanotan II, GHK-Cu, KPV, MOTS-c, epitalon, DSIP, selank, semax and retatrutide. Most are
  unapproved everywhere, so S0 **arguably** captures them; that is my interpretation and not a WADA ruling.
  Semax and selank reportedly hold Russian approvals (UNVERIFIED), which may take them outside S0. MOTS-c may fall
  under S4.4 as an AMPK activator. Athletes should treat every one of these as prohibited unless Sport Integrity
  Australia says otherwise.
- **Detection.** Anti-doping labs have validated methods for BPC-157 in urine (Cox2017). Vendor claims that
  "there is no test" are false.
- **Athlete cases:**
  - USADA v Lute (2024): **1-year ban from a contaminated supplement** containing BPC-157 and DHEA, although the
    athlete never tested positive. Supplement contamination alone ended a season.
  - CCES (2024–25): BPC-157 declared alongside an adverse analytical finding for steroids and SARMs.
  - NZ Sports Tribunal: ineligible until December 2027 in a case involving BPC-157 (details fragmentary).

## 6. Peptide clinics and telehealth: conflicts of interest

- These clinics prescribe and sell the product they recommend, often with a subscription. **Their efficacy
  claims, testimonials and "protocols" are marketing (evidence level: anecdote/marketing), not evidence.**
- The FDA's 2024–26 reversals were triggered by **nominators withdrawing**. Most nominators are compounding
  pharmacies or industry groups. Much of the online commentary on the 2026 changes comes from compounding
  pharmacies, med-spa associations and peptide vendors, all of whom benefit from the change. I discounted those
  sources and relied on the law-firm summaries (McDermott, Goodwin, Buchanan Ingersoll, Frier Levitt), which are
  themselves written for industry clients.
- The Finnrick testing dataset is used commercially to rate vendors, which is a second-order conflict for anyone
  citing it.
- Australian context: the 2019 Federal Court found **no medical justification** for the uses one clinic advertised.
  The 2026 analysis describes clinics using patient-named compounding to avoid pre-market assessment.

## 7. Theoretical cancer and growth-promotion risk

- **GH/IGF-1 axis** (ipamorelin, CJC-1295, GHRPs, sermorelin, tesamorelin, MK-677). In UK Biobank, higher
  *endogenous* IGF-1 is associated with breast cancer (HR 1.11 per 5 nmol/L; Mendelian-randomisation OR 1.05) and
  colorectal cancer (HR 1.11 per SD; MR OR 1.08), and the authors judged the link probably causal. Evidence: human
  observational plus genetic data; moderate that the association exists, **very limited for any transfer to an
  exogenous secretagogue**. The effects are modest per unit, and the dose-equivalence for a peptide user is unknown.
  No prostate result was retrieved.
- **BPC-157 and TB-500.** The proposed benefits work through angiogenesis and cell migration (VEGFR2 for BPC-157,
  actin and cell migration for Tβ4). In a person with an undiagnosed tumour, the same mechanism is a plausible way
  to promote growth. **No human tumour-outcome data exist, and no peer-reviewed review of this was found.** Score it
  as *unknown*, not as *absent*.
- **Epitalon.** The marketed claim is telomerase activation. Telomerase activation is a hallmark of cancer cells,
  and it has not been tested for risk in humans. Unknown.

## 8. Immunogenicity and anti-drug antibodies

- Even pharmaceutical-grade peptides raise antibodies. **Tirzepatide: treatment-emergent ADA in 51.1%** of 5,025
  phase-3 patients, neutralising in about 2%, with no effect on PK or efficacy, but more injection-site reactions
  (Tirzepatide_ADA_2024, Lilly-run, ABSTRACT).
- The FDA's case against compounded and grey-market peptides rests on immunogenicity. Peptide-related impurities
  can redirect antibodies, and immune-activating impurities matter "even at trace levels" (FDA_PCAC_immuno2024).
  Aggregation is cited specifically for epitalon and GHK-Cu.
- The serious tail is anaphylaxis (Victorian cluster, §2) and, in principle, antibodies cross-reacting with an
  endogenous hormone. I found no documented cross-reactivity case for any gym peptide; it is a theoretical
  mechanism only.

Benefit and adverse-event evidence for each substance belongs to P1–P4. P5 adds only regulatory status and

- **BPC-157.** TGA: S4 + Appendix D, not on the ARTG. WADA: S0, named. FDA: unapproved; Category 2 from 2023,

- **MK-677 (non-peptide).** TGA: S4 + Appendix D as a GHS. WADA: S2.2.4, named. Unapproved everywhere.
- **AOD-9604.** WADA: S2 GH fragment, named. FDA: left Category 2 in 2024. TGA: not verified.

- **KPV, MOTS-c, epitalon, semax.** FDA: Category 2 to removal to PCAC recommendation (2026). WADA: not named
  (S0 or S4.4 is arguable). TGA: not verified.

- **Selank, thymosin alpha-1.** Left FDA Category 2 in 2024. Foreign approvals reported but UNVERIFIED.
- **Melanotan II.** TGA: S4 since 2018; Schedule 9 claim unconfirmed. Case reports of naevi and melanoma. Supply
  documented as mislabelled. WADA: not named.
- **Bremelanotide.** FDA-approved; not on the WADA List.
- **Semaglutide, tirzepatide.** Approved (FDA, TGA). Not prohibited by WADA. The online and counterfeit products
  are where the quality failures are (Ashraf2024).
- **Retatrutide.** Investigational. Victorian cluster of 6 liver injuries from a product labelled retatrutide.
  Named in TGA alerts.
- **LL-37, dihexa, PEG-MGF.** Category 2, PCAC review due before February 2027. MGF variants have been found in
  confiscated vials.

## 10. Proposed scoring rubric for the three rankings

The rubric is designed so that a substance with little data cannot score well on safety, and so that marketing
cannot push a benefit score up.

### (a) Research volume, score 0–6 (higher means more researched)

| points | criterion (count only human studies of the substance itself) |
|---|---|
| +3 | approved by FDA, EMA or TGA for some indication (the regulator has seen a full dossier) |
| +1 | a phase-3 programme is complete or under way (not cumulative with approval if approval came from that programme) |
| +2 | ≥10 human RCTs **or** ≥3,000 RCT participants |
| +1 | 3–9 human RCTs **or** 300–2,999 RCT participants |
| +0.5 | 1–2 human RCTs, or any controlled human study |
| 0 | human case reports or uncontrolled pilots only (BPC-157's n=2 pilot sits here) |

Tie-breakers, in order: total RCT participants; longest RCT follow-up; PubMed hits (secondary indicator only).
RCTs in a different population (for example, diabetes trials for a fat-loss claim in lean lifters) count toward
volume and are flagged as indirect.

### (b) Benefit strength, graded A–E per outcome, then the best outcome taken

| grade | definition |
|---|---|
| A | ≥2 independent RCTs or a meta-analysis, consistent, clinically meaningful effect size **in the population claimed** (e.g. % body-weight loss in adults with obesity) |
| B | ≥1 good RCT with a meaningful effect, or consistent RCTs in an adjacent population (indirect) |
| C | small or short RCTs, surrogate outcomes only (e.g. GH/IGF-1 rise without body-composition or performance change), or mixed results |
| D | human non-randomised or case data only, or animal/in-vitro only |
| E | anecdote or marketing only, or RCTs showing no effect |

Downgrade one grade for industry-only funding with no independent replication, for a gym claim that rests on a
clinical population, or for a sole source run by a clinic or vendor. Record the effect size and population next to
the grade; a letter on its own is not enough.

### (c) Side effects, scored on two axes and never collapsed silently

**Severity S** (worst plausible outcome with evidence): S1 mild and self-limiting (nausea, injection-site
reaction); S2 moderate (needs treatment or stopping; e.g. gallbladder events, glucose rise); S3 serious
(hospitalisation, anaphylaxis, organ injury); S4 life-threatening or irreversible (malignancy, death).

**Characterisation C** (how well the side effects are known):
- C1: RCT safety databases of 1,000 or more people plus post-marketing surveillance.
- C2: RCT data on 100–999 people.
- C3: fewer than 100 people in controlled studies, or case reports only.
- **CU (unknown):** essentially no controlled human safety data.

**Supply modifier Q:**
- Q0: pharmaceutical-grade supply available.
- Q1: compounded only.
- Q2: grey market only, with documented purity, endotoxin or mislabelling problems. Q2 adds S3 (infection,
  anaphylaxis, wrong drug) as a floor, whatever the molecule.

**Ranking rule for "mildest/best-characterised to most serious/least known":** sort by C first, then S, then Q.
A substance at **CU must rank below every C1–C2 substance, whatever its apparent severity**, because "no reported
side effects" in an unstudied drug is the absence of data. Within CU, rank by any mechanistic red flag
(IGF-1/angiogenesis/telomerase gives a "theoretical S4" tag) and by Q.

Worked placement, illustrative and for the coordinator to confirm with P1–P4 data:
- Semaglutide and tirzepatide: C1, S2 with rare S3, Q0. They rank as best characterised, though not the mildest.
- Tesamorelin: C1–C2, Q0.
- GH secretagogues: C2–C3, theoretical S4 (IGF-1), Q2.
- BPC-157, TB-500, KPV, MOTS-c, epitalon, GHK-Cu (injected), DSIP, dihexa: CU, Q2, with theoretical S4 flags
  where noted. These go at the bottom.
- Melanotan II: C3 (case reports), S4 (melanoma, unproven), Q2.
